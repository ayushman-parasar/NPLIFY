1 · Three roles, one principle

Every controlled action is maker-checker: one person initiates, a different person in the approving role approves, and the system refuses an approval by the initiator. Since the rights are configuration, not code: ROLE holds the three roles of today and ROLE_RIGHT says per controlled action which role may initiate, which may approve and which is added on escalation; the approver's role must hold may_approve for the action. Visibility is cumulative: Operations sees the least, Finance sees everything Operations sees plus the economics, Management sees everything.

2 · The deal: Quote → Collect → Convert → Settle

A deal is a group of one or more legs. A quote-first leg walks the whole top row of Figure 1; a collect-first leg is born at Collected because the money arrived before any quote; an estimate-first leg enters Collecting with an indicative figure and is priced at conversion; a return leg is born at Collected, funded by the returned payout and attributed to its parent leg's group. Pricing fields are stamped once and never recomputed. Fee parts are percentages of the principal at the basis rate and never compound; a target-out quote applies the fee as a markup on the target.

2a · Effective-date rules

Pricing stamps are write-once, and configuration is versioned by effective date (Configuration Schema, section 1). The four rules below say which version a deal uses; they are the same rules as in the Configuration Schema.

RuleWhat happens
1. New versions apply to new pricing onlyAn approved configuration change takes effect for deals priced on or after its effective date. A deal priced before that date is not re-priced.
2. A priced deal keeps its versionsThe fee structure version, the tier, the rate basis, the rounding options, the partner configuration and the policy versions stamped at pricing stay with the deal to the end of its life, including through settlement, return and loss handling.
3. A re-quote uses the configuration of the new pricing timeAn expired or requoted leg is priced afresh on the versions in force when the new quote is made (a new deal group); the original leg keeps its stamp.
4. A collect-first deal uses the configuration in force when it is pricedA collect-first leg is priced at conversion, so it uses the versions effective at that time, not at the time the money arrived, unless a documented client agreement names a different rule for that project.

2c · Holds: scope, allowed actions, release and audit

A hold is a named condition on a deal leg, not a narrative. It comes from one of two sources and keeps the leg in the state it was in: a partner hold (the partner, or its bank, reports a hold, freeze or decline on the client's money or on a destination: NPL records it as the event it is and does not decide it) or a screening hold (NPL's own screening of a wallet on the leg is not clear under the provider's Strict rulebook and awaits Management's second look, or a decision of reject stands). NPL has no compliance function; the partner's decisions are the partner's, and the only decision NPL takes is Management's second look on its own screening (Roles & Visibility rule 11).

QuestionRule
ScopeThe hold attaches to the leg and names what it covers: the collected money (partner hold on the balance, or a screening hold on the sender's wallet), or the destination (partner freeze of the destination, or a screening hold on the receiver's wallet). A partner hold on a balance covers every leg whose money rests in it.
Blocked while it standsEvery action that moves the held money or pays the held destination: instructing a conversion or a balance conversion, preparing or releasing a settlement line, a refund or sender credit paid out, a reroute forward from custody, a return leg. The server refuses them with the hold as the reason, whichever role asks and whichever channel (screen, API, a replayed request).
Allowed while it standsRecording and evidence: a new screening row, partner messages and their references, the exception list, verification of a part that has already landed, a void before money moved on a leg that has not collected, and settlement lines of other legs that neither draw on the held money nor pay the held destination.
Who lifts itA partner hold: nobody at NPL; it ends when the partner's clearance is recorded with its reference (Finance records it; it is an audited record, not an approval). A screening hold: Management's second-look decision accept or accept_alternate (an APPROVAL under screening_second_look, approver never the person who screened), or a new screening row that is clear. Finance has no right over either.
DispositionWhile a hold stands, the only money movement is the one the partner instructs in writing (a refund to the sender's return receiver, or a transfer the partner directs); it is recorded with the partner's reference and approved by Management, and never happens while a partner hold is on the balance itself. The reviewer's case, funds from a newly flagged wallet and a partner asking for a refund, is exactly this path: Operations cannot issue the return transfer; Management approves it on the partner's instruction.
Expiry and re-screeningA hold does not expire by time. A screening hold is re-examined only by a new screening row (new report, new decision); a partner hold is chased daily from the exception list with its age (Section 8). A leg on hold for longer than the project's limit is escalated to Management by the time-limit rule of 8a.
AuditThe start and the end of every hold are AUDIT_LOG rows naming the event or screening row, the reference, the actor and the time; the second-look decision is an APPROVAL; every refused action under a hold is logged with the hold as its reason. The ERD gains the hold on the leg (hold_kind, hold_ref) and the tests of the Test Strategy (S-HOLD) attempt a payout, a conversion, a refund and a reroute under each kind of hold.

2d · External instructions: prepared, acknowledged, executed

Every instruction NPL gives a partner — a conversion, a balance conversion, a settlement line, the forward of a reroute — is one row with its own reference (instruction_ref, the idempotency key of the Architecture) and an acknowledgement status that distinguishes three facts the reviewer asks to keep apart: the instruction is prepared at NPL, the partner has acknowledged it (from then on it cannot be withdrawn: the first point of no return), and the partner has executed it (the money moved: the second). A line is "sent" only on the partner's acknowledgement; an instruction with no acknowledgement is "outcome unknown" and stays so until the partner's statement or confirmation says executed or not executed. The two held states are different things: "sent, not yet confirmed" (Ledger Posting Design rule 13a) is an acknowledged instruction waiting for its execution to be confirmed, and posts nothing; "sent, outcome unknown" is an instruction the partner has not acknowledged at all. A line enters "outcome unknown" when the partner's acknowledgement has not arrived by the acknowledgement time-out of the instruction (the partner's configured wait after the call; a failed call counts the same), and Operations raises a partner reference inquiry the same day, recording the partner's answer with its evidence on the instruction.

  1. One open instruction per promise. While an instruction for a promise (a settlement line, a conversion of a balance) is instructed, acknowledged or of unknown outcome, the server refuses a second instruction for the same promise from any role and any channel: the unique key is the promise, not the request (Ledger Posting Design rule 1b, Pattern P). This is what prevents a double payment.
  2. A reroute or any alternative payout starts from a closed instruction. A reroute after conversion, a re-instructed settlement line or a payout through another partner is allowed only when the original instruction's status is rejected, or acknowledged as not executed, with the partner's reference recorded on the instruction, and the balance reconciliation shows the client's money still at the first partner (ledger balance equal to the partner's statement, the amount included).
  3. Unknown is not rejected. An instruction with outcome unknown is on the exception list with its age; Finance obtains the partner's statement or written confirmation; nothing is re-instructed meanwhile. Management may authorise an exception only with the partner's written confirmation that the instruction was not and will not be executed, its reference, and the reconciliation attached; the exception is an APPROVAL naming them, and the instruction is closed as rejected before the alternative is instructed. Three ways out, each recorded on the held instruction: executed — the partner's statement or reference shows the money moved; the instruction is marked acknowledged and executed against that reference and the line continues as usual, nothing posted twice; not received — the partner confirms in writing that it never received the instruction and will not execute it; Management closes the instruction as rejected on that confirmation (the exception above) and Finance authorises the one retry under a new partner reference, recorded as a retry of the held instruction (retry_of), never a second open instruction; answered late — the acknowledgement arrives after the time-out; it is recorded as the acknowledgement with its real time and the line proceeds as acknowledged. The Test Strategy test G-EXT is this rule at acceptance level.
  4. Delayed confirmation is not failure. The reviewer's case, the first partner debits EUR but confirmation is delayed, is an acknowledged instruction: the alternative payout is refused until the partner's statement arrives, and when it shows the money moved the line ends confirmed as usual. The Test Strategy test S-ACK exercises every acknowledgement outcome (acknowledged late, rejected, unknown then executed, unknown then not executed) and proves no second instruction can be created while the first is open.

2e · Email OTP: the customer's approval of the order

Today a customer's consent to an order is a message in a chat group that the system never sees. Email OTP is an optional feature, switched on per project, that makes that consent a recorded step of the deal: after the quote is prepared (and Finance has approved it where the thresholds require), the customer's registered approver receives one email with the order as quoted and a one-time code, pastes the code in that sender's registered chat group (each sender has its own group and its own approver address), and Operations enters it. The code is the acceptance. In the states of Figure 1 nothing new is needed: Sent is the quote awaiting the customer's code, Accepted is the code validated; the screens label them pending customer approval and customer approved, and the approval row carries its own stamps. Projects with the feature off follow today's flow unchanged. The design comes from the reviewer's change request CR-01 of October 2026 and is a priced change to the build scope.

QuestionRule
What the email carriesThe quote issue as rendered (deal reference with the issue number, client, pair, amount in, the sender-facing rate at five decimals, fees, amount out, collection method, validity), a one-time code, its expiry in the project's time zone, and, where the collection destination exists at that point, a partial destination: the first and last four characters of the collection wallet, or the last four digits of the receiving account; nothing for cash collection, where the token at handover is the control. The instruction printed: paste the code in the registered chat group to approve; reply with 'reject' and the reason to reject; the full destination will follow in the registered group and must match the partial above; NPL never creates new groups. The code box states that the code is for this quote issue only, is used once, and is void if any detail of the order changes. At or above the project's step-up amount the instruction reads 'paste the code and the order amount in the registered chat group'; below that amount the clause is omitted.
What it never carriesA link or button that approves or rejects (mail scanners open links, and a link invites a swapped destination), the full wallet or bank account, or anything of the hidden field groups. The email is information and a code; the approval happens across two channels the customer already has, the registered inbox and the registered chat group. A cloned chat group cannot produce the email leg, cannot use a code (codes approve only the real issue, entered by Operations from the registered group) and cannot substitute a destination that survives the partial-identifier check.
The codeEight or more characters from an unambiguous alphabet, generated at random, stored only as a hash, bound to the deal group, the quote issue and the approver address it was sent to, valid for the project's code window (default 24 hours), consumed on its first successful validation. Five failed attempts lock it; a locked, expired or voided code needs a new send. A code is never shown on any screen or report.
Step-up for large ordersAt or above the project's step-up amount (default USD 50,000 equivalent), the customer's paste must carry the code and the order amount, and Operations enters both; a code alone does not validate.
One issue, one codeThe code approves exactly one quote issue. Any change to the order after sending (amount, rate, fees, method, destination) and any resend is a new issue: a new email, a new code, the old code void. The emailed snapshot is stored with the deal, content-addressed like a bank slip. Exactly one email per issue.
The two clocksThe quote's validity decides the price; the code window decides how long the customer has. Inside validity the approval counts at the quoted price. After validity but inside the code window it counts only if the rate has moved less than the project's drift tolerance at that moment (grace_drift_pct, the existing rule); otherwise a new issue. After the code window the code is expired, the group is back at Quoted and Operations is alerted. The screens show a countdown on the work list and raise three alerts: a code nearing expiry, a rejection received, and a change to a sender's approver address or chat group pending Finance's approval.
TripwireIf a code is issued and nothing from the registered chat group has been recorded against it within the tripwire window (default 4 hours), an alert tells Operations to verify with the customer through a known contact outside the chat. A validated code whose chat evidence cannot be produced from the registered group blocks collection and opens an exception.
RejectionA reply to the email from the registered approver address, or a message in the registered chat group, is recorded as a rejection with its reason verbatim; the group leaves Sent (re-quoted as a new group, or voided). Nothing that arrives by email ever approves; a reply from any other address is recorded and ignored.
Evidence and auditThe approval record carries the stored email snapshot, the code reference, the validation time, the entering user and the chat screenshot; a rejection carries its reason. Every send, resend, delivery failure, bounce, reply, validation attempt, lock, void and tripwire is an event on the approval and an AUDIT_LOG row.
The settings, protectedThe project holds the switch and the three windows; each sender holds exactly one approver email and one registered chat group (platform, group name, group id), because every sender has its own chat group with NPL. The requirement is per sender: email_otp_required, true by default while the switch is on. While the switch is on, a quote for a required sender without an approved address and group cannot be sent, and the screen says why. A customer who will not name an approver address is marked not required: that sender is quoted and approved in its registered chat group as today, and every quote issue sent to it records 'approval taken in chat, Email OTP not required for this sender' on the quote, the deal and the audit log. Marking a sender not required removes a control, so Operations enters, Finance approves and Management approves with a recorded reason, and the exemption is listed on the quarterly review. The three windows are THRESHOLD rows and follow the threshold rule: Finance proposes, Management approves. The switch and the sender's settings change through set_email_otp_settings: Operations enters, Finance approves, never the same person; a new approver address activates only after a verification code sent to it comes back through that sender's chat group; while a change is pending no approval email can be sent for that sender; a change of address voids that sender's open codes only, so a live code never moves; the sender row shows the current address, the audit log the history. Switching the feature off is the same controlled change; groups already pending complete or expire under the rules in force when sent (Roles & Visibility rule 13; Configuration Schema rule 16).

Operations sends the full deposit wallet or account only after the approval, in the registered chat group, and the customer's standing rule is to pay only if it matches the partial identifier in the email. The acceptance test is S-OTP (Test Strategy).

NPL-GR's two invoices

In the reseller project NPL-GR is the client of its own remittance deal. The vendor's invoice to NPL-GR and NPL-GR's resale invoice to its customer are posted once each, in the invoice currency, when received and when issued; the resale invoice names the vendor invoice it marks up. A deal group that collects the resale invoice names it and the part it collects (advance, then balance) at the pricing rate; each vendor payment line names the vendor invoice, the obligation it discharges and the rate at discharge, and the forex result of paying on a later day's rate is a true-up on the remittance side, never on the reseller revenue. A resale invoice is settled when its groups have collected its amount; a vendor invoice when its released lines have discharged its amount (INVOICE_BALANCE by kind).

6 · Rejection handling, the same way everywhere

  1. Nothing is deleted. The request, the rejection and its reason stay on the APPROVAL record and in the audit log. A re-submission is a new approval request that points at the rejected one.
  2. The record returns to the initiator's last safe state. A rejected quote is not sent; a rejected settlement goes back to Prepared; a rejected reroute leaves the route unchanged; a rejected balance conversion leaves the balance in its currency; a rejected offset leaves every margin where it rests.
  3. Money that already moved is never rolled back by a rejection. A conversion the partner has booked stands; a variance exception is resolved by a decision and, if needed, a reversing ledger entry, not by editing the conversion. A loss that Management has approved stands as NPL's; only its shares are recovered.
  4. A rejection reason is mandatory and is visible to the initiator; derived-economics fields are stripped from the reason shown to Operations.
  5. Frozen states need a decision, not time. A breached rate version, an open variance exception, an open offset, custody in NPL's wallet, an unfunded make-good and a negative pool all stay on the daily exception list until a role with authority acts. A leg on hold (2c) and an instruction with outcome unknown (2d) are on the same list and end the same way: by a recorded decision or reference, never by time.
  6. Escalation never skips a level. Management approves in addition to Finance; when only one Finance user is on shift, Management's countersignature stands in for the second Finance user.

6a · Concurrency rules

Section 5 of the Architecture note already gives every money-moving action a unique key, so a retried request cannot post twice, and locks the record a person is editing. Three rules complete that protection.

RuleWhat the system does
1. An approval is valid only for the state the approver sawAn APPROVAL carries the record version it was requested on. If the record changed between request and approval (another user edited it, a job moved it, a second approval landed), the approval is refused and the approver is shown the current state; a fresh request is needed. Two approvers acting on the same request at once: the first commit wins, the second is refused.
2. Two runs of a job never overlapEach background job of the Architecture note (Section 4) takes a lock row before it runs and releases it at the end; a run that finds the lock held exits at once and is recorded as skipped. After a restart the job catches up from where the last run finished, so nothing is processed twice.
3. A record lock expiresRECORD_LOCK is released on save or cancel; it expires after a fixed idle time (THRESHOLD kind lock_idle_minutes, default 15) so a closed laptop does not block a record for the day; Management can clear a lock; the lock, its release, its expiry and any clearance are written to AUDIT_LOG. The lock row carries lock_expires_at (ERD Appendix B.5).

These rules are built in Phase 1 (M3 to M5) and tested with the concurrency, duplicate-submission and outage cases of the go-live acceptance tests (Cutover Approach, exit criteria 9 to 13).

8 · Aging and the daily exception list

These things age and are reported daily with deal, reason and hours since the event: a quote awaiting the customer's code (Email OTP), with the hours left in its code window and whether the tripwire has passed; client money in NPL's own wallet (custody — reroutes, bridge collections awaiting their hop, own-desk balances), open offsets across receiver groups, open shortfalls, open variance exceptions, a pool below zero awaiting the next conversion, an unfunded make-good (MAKEGOOD / LOSS_PAYABLE), loss recoveries agreed and not received, and settlement lines sent beyond the rail's expected time, legs on hold (partner or screening) with the hold's age and source, and external instructions whose outcome is unknown. Breached rate versions, frozen partner × pairs and quotes past their validity appear on the same list. Time limits for each are project configuration (THRESHOLD, OWN_WALLET.custody_max_hours) and are not fixed by this document.
