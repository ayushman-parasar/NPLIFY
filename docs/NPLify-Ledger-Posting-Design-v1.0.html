1 · What the ledger is for, in one paragraph

NPL coordinates other people's money. The ledger's job is to answer, at any moment and for any past moment, three questions: where is the money (at which partner, in transit, in NPL's own wallet), whose money is it (the client's, or NPL's earnings), and what did each movement cost (NPL's margin, the partner's cut, bank and network fees, variances). It does this with ordinary double-entry bookkeeping: every movement is a set of debits and credits that balance, nothing is ever edited or deleted, and every balance anyone sees is a sum over those entries.

2 · Rules that every posting follows

  1. One transaction per event. A collection, a conversion, a disbursement, a return, a confirmation, a bank-fee event, a reroute, a referral or rebate accrual, or a manual adjustment each produce exactly one LEDGER_TRANSACTION that points back at its source record.
  2. Balanced per currency. Within one transaction the debits equal the credits in each currency separately. A conversion therefore has a USDT part and a EUR part that each balance on their own; no exchange-rate "plug" is ever needed.
  3. Append-only. A mistake is corrected by a reversing transaction that names the one it reverses, followed by the correct transaction. Reports for any past date still add up.
  4. Every posting carries an owner tag: client (the client's funds) or company (NPL's earnings and costs). Client money and company money never sit in the same account.
  5. Every posting carries a cost component: principal, earnings, partner_cost, bank_fee, network_fee, variance, referral or rounding. Summing a component across postings gives the cost breakdown of any deal, day, partner or client.
  6. Ownership flips from client to company at one event only: margin recognition at conversion. Money flows the other way (company to client) only when NPL absorbs a fee or tops up a shortfall. There is no withholding of earnings at source and NPL never advances money to a receiver, so neither has a posting pattern.
  7. Nothing is stored as a balance. Client balance per partner, group entitlement, offsets, custody and exposure are all sums over postings or deals.

3 · Chart of accounts

Account codes read OWNER.HOLDER.CURRENCY.PURPOSE. The owner is CL for client funds or CO for company. The holder says where the money is or whom it concerns: a partner code such as PT or JETON (a PARTNER_CONFIG, so always within one project), OWN for NPL's own wallet, a receiver, a sender, an introducer, PROJECT for the client as a whole, or NONE for income and expense accounts. One account exists per combination that is actually used; accounts are created on first posting.

The ERD's purpose list gains PAYABLE and WALLET as used here and the holder type Project; ADVANCE is not used because NPL never advances money. Rebates are credited to EXP_PARTNER and referral commissions debited to EARN_GROSS, so no extra income or expense accounts are needed.

7 · Pattern C — accumulated and instant settlement, bank fees, shortfalls

There is one settlement model. "Instant" simply means the balance is disbursed as soon as one deal has accumulated; "accumulated" means several deals first. The postings are the same. Disbursements pay whole units; the cents below one unit stay in the client balance and go out when they add up to a unit.

The three bank-fee treatments on a 50 EUR fee. Waived: the client bears it; the confirmation debits CL.PROJECT.EUR.PAYABLE for the full released amount and the client's claim simply ends 50 lower. Carry-forward: T8 and T9, the fee becomes a shortfall that NPL funds from its pool and adds to the next disbursement. Absorbed (gross-up): NPL releases 50 more than the client net so the receiver nets the right amount; at release Dr CO.NONE.EUR.EXP_BANKFEE 50 / Cr CO.PT.EUR.POOL 50 is posted alongside the client's in-transit line, and a BANK_FEE_EVENT later trues up expected against actual. The project sets the default; one settlement can override it with an approved FEE_DECISION.

8 · Pattern D — reroute through NPL's own wallet, with dues recovered

This is the only time NPL holds client money. When the intended partner cannot take the volume, an approved REROUTE collects into NPL's own wallet and forwards to an alternate partner. In v4.0 there is no withholding of earnings at source: NPL's margin is captured when the alternate partner converts, exactly as in Pattern A. What a reroute can do is recover dues: net what the alternate partner already owes NPL against the money being forwarded.

9 · Pattern E — cancellations and reversals

  • Void before any money moved: a state change with a reason; no postings.
  • Erroneous posting: never edited. A reversing transaction mirrors it and names it; the correct transaction follows (T13, T14).
  • Void after collection: the money goes back to the sender (network-fee policy decides who bears the fee), is kept as sender credit, or is held; Management approves the disposition (T15).
  • Rejected payout: the released line comes back to the partner minus the bank's fee; the line is reversed into the client balance and the fee is booked (T18). The money then waits for a later payout or funds a return leg (T19).

11 · Pattern G — inter-recipient offsets

An offset is not a ledger event. When a disbursement line pays a receiver in another receiver group than the one entitled to the money, the postings are exactly T6 and T7; the ledger sees client money leaving the client balance and reaching a receiver. What changes is the GROUP_ENTITLEMENT view: the paid group now shows less entitlement than it should (or a negative figure), the entitled group shows more, and the two differences are equal. The offset clears as later collections for the entitled group are converted and later payouts favour it. Within one group, paying any receiver or entity is ordinary settlement and nothing is tracked beyond an informational tally. Offsets across groups carry Management approval and tighter aging.

Advances. NPL never pays a receiver before the client's money has arrived, so there is no advance account and no advance posting pattern in this design.

12 · Pattern H — balance conversion

NPL may convert a client's held balance from one currency to another to fund a payout, for example 5,000 SGD to USDT at PT. It is not a deal: there is no sender, receiver or collection, and Finance requests and approves it. The posting moves the client's asset and obligation from one currency to the other; a markup, if a FEE_DECISION applies one, posts to the pool and to gross earnings as in T2.

17 · Who sees what

Operations sees operational fields, sender-facing prices and partner rates as entered, but never a ledger balance, a margin, a pool, a cost breakdown or a variance, on any screen, export, message or report. Finance sees all postings and balances and owns reconciliation. Management additionally approves settlements above limit, cross-group payouts, shortfall top-ups, contingency routes, own-wallet outbound transfers, balance conversions, dues recovery and post-money voids. Approver is never the initiator, and every controlled action is audited with actor, time, reason and record version.
