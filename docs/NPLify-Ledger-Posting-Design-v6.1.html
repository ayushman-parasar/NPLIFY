1 · What the ledger is for, in one paragraph

NPL coordinates other people's money and, in one project, is also its own client: NPL-GR resells goods and pays for the remittance like any customer. The ledger answers, at any moment and for any past moment, three questions: where is the money (at which partner, sent and awaiting confirmation, in one of NPL's wallets), whose money is it (the client's, a sender's, or NPL's), and what did each movement cost or earn (NPL's margin and shares of it, rebates, partner cost, bank and network fees, rounding, losses, and the four kinds of variance). It does this with ordinary double-entry bookkeeping: every movement is a set of debits and credits that balance, nothing is ever edited or deleted, and every balance anyone sees is a sum over those entries.

2 · Rules that every posting follows

  1. 1.One transaction per event. A collection, a conversion, a balance conversion, a settlement, a settlement return, a confirmation, a bank-fee event, a reroute, a markup-share or rebate accrual or settlement, a loss event or recovery, and a manual adjustment each produce exactly one LEDGER_TRANSACTION that points at its source record (source_type, source_id). A source row with a lifecycle — a share or rebate accrual, a loss recovery — produces one transaction per state change, each naming the same row; a full confirmation and a hop release are events that post nothing (rule 13).
  2. 1a.One event, one database transaction. The source record's state change and its balanced postings are committed together or not at all: a conversion is not Booked unless its LEDGER_TRANSACTION exists, and no LEDGER_TRANSACTION exists for an event that did not change state. A retried event carrying the same idempotency key (Architecture note, Section 5) finds the transaction already posted and posts nothing; a replay test in Phase 1 (M2) proves it for every source type of Section 22.
  3. 1b.How 1a is enforced in the database, and what a failure leaves behind. Four mechanisms, all at database level (ERD Appendix C). (1) Every LEDGER_TRANSACTION carries the idempotency key of the action that posts it, under a unique index, and the pair (source_type, source_id, event) is unique as well: a retried action, with the same key on a new transport request, is refused by the index and the application returns the first result; two different events can never share a transaction. (2) The source record's state change, the transaction row and its postings are written in one database transaction, so either all three exist or none; the deferred constraint trigger checks at commit that the postings balance per currency and per owner, and refuses the whole transaction otherwise. (3) A conversion is marked Booked, a settlement Released, only inside that same transaction, never before it: there is no code path that sets the state and posts afterwards. (4) The one failure the database cannot prevent is the external one: the partner was instructed and acted, and NPLify's commit failed afterwards. That case is never an orphan, because the instruction itself is committed first, in its own transaction, before the partner is called or told (an API call, or the message that instructs a partner who works by hand): a conversion in state Converting or a settlement line with outcome sent and the partner reference already stored. What the failure leaves is a reconcilable state — an instruction with no posting — which the exception list shows past its expected time (Pattern P, case 1) and which Operations repairs by recording the partner's outcome once, under the original key. The chaos test of the Test Strategy (C-ATOM) injects a failure at every step of every posting path and checks that no source record is left in a state its posting does not match and that no transaction is ever duplicated.
  4. 2.Balanced per currency and per owner. Within one transaction the debits equal the credits in each currency separately, and separately again for client-tagged and company-tagged lines. A conversion therefore has an in-currency part and an out-currency part that each balance on their own; no exchange-rate plug is ever needed, and nothing silently moves between the client's books and NPL's.
  5. 3.Ownership is a tag on every line (ownership_tag client / company) and the account code says the same (CL. / CO.). Ownership moves at named events only. Client → company: margin recognition at conversion; the client side of an earnings offset; a forex true-up gain on an invoice line; the client's share of a loss. Company → client: a fee NPL absorbs or covers, a round-up, a shortfall top-up, a bounce make-whole, a loss make-good; the company side of an earnings offset; a forex true-up loss. Each transaction balances per owner, so these movements are always explicit lines.
  6. 4.A cost component on every line (cost_component): principal, earnings, rebate, partner_cost, bank_fee, network_fee, variance, share, rounding, fx_timing, cutoff_timing, loss, and resale for the reseller project. Reports of margin, cost and variance are sums over this tag; no figure is stored.
  7. 5.Accruals post with the conversion. NPL's margin, each markup-share party's part and the partner rebate expected on the leg are all recognised at conversion, as separate transactions that name the same deal; the monthly statement settles them and any difference is a true-up, never a rewrite.
  8. 6.Variances are booked by kind, to four accounts: VAR_CONVERSION (the partner did something other than expected), VAR_CUTOFF, VAR_RATE_HONOUR, VAR_FX_TIMING (instalments and the own desk). Each can be a gain or a loss; each line names the leg or line it arose on.
  9. 7.A loss is NPL's first. A LOSS_EVENT posts the whole amount to EXP_LOSS and makes the client whole in the same transaction; each share the partner, the sender or the client agrees to bear is a separate, later recovery posting.
  10. 8.NPL-GR is the client of its own remittance project. Its reseller books are two postings per deal in the invoice currency, one per invoice, and a claim on the remittance side; the remittance deal posts like any client project; the forex result of paying the vendor in instalments is a true-up to VAR_FX_TIMING against NPL's pool, so the reseller revenue is never diluted.
  11. 9.Earnings may be kept in the collection currency and offset between partners. A conversion may keep the deal's margin unconverted (residual_in) and an approved EARNINGS_OFFSET may swap NPL's margin at one partner for the same value of unconverted client money at another, at a stated rate; the client's entitlement never changes, and NPL's exchange of one currency for another is recorded in FX_RESULT (Pattern N).
  12. 10.The views are sums over postings. PROJECT_BALANCE is the balance of the DUE account per partner; GROUP_ENTITLEMENT is the sum of PAYABLE postings by receiver_group_id, which every promise line carries; neither is computed from deals.
  13. 11.Append-only. A correction is a reversing transaction that names what it reverses (reversal_of_id) followed by the correct posting.
  14. 11a.Corrections never move a reported month. A correction is posted on the day it is made, with a reference to the month and the source record it corrects (LEDGER_TRANSACTION.corrects_period, the month in YYYY-MM form), never back-dated. A month that has been reported to NPL's accountant therefore never changes after it was handed over; the correction appears in the month it was made, labelled with the month it belongs to. Reports can be run by deal date or by posting date, so Finance sees both views. There is no period-close feature in P0; if the parallel run shows that Finance needs a formal month lock, it is added in Phase 2 (M7).
  15. 12.Amounts stay in their own currency. A converted total (volumes, earnings, balances, custody in the reporting currency) is a REPORTING_VALUE: computed at read time from the latest platform-pair market rate, carrying that rate and its time, flagged when stale, and never posted, quoted or settled.
  16. 13.Sent is discharged. A settlement line discharges the promise when the partner releases the money; the receiver's confirmation posts nothing, a short delivery re-raises the promise for the missing part, and a return re-raises it for what came back. What is sent and unconfirmed is the line's outcome (SETTLEMENT_LINE.outcome sent), not a jar; every released line ends confirmed, short or returned.
  17. 13a.Confirmed by NPL. NPL Finance confirmed rule 13 on 20 October 2026 and accepted that “sent, not yet confirmed” is a named state of the settlement line, shown on the settlement screens and on a Finance log report (Phase 2, M7) that lists every released line with its release time, the rail's expected confirmation time and its age. The state is still read from the line (SETTLEMENT_LINE.outcome sent, with released_at and expected_confirmation_by), never from an account.
  18. 14.Rounding has an owner. Bank transfers go out exact, cents included (amount_rounding exact); rounding to a unit applies where the pair says so, in practice to cash hand-overs. A dropped fraction stays client money in the balance; an amount added by rounding up is NPL's cost in EXP_ROUNDING.

3 · Chart of accounts

Account codes read OWNER.HOLDER.CURRENCY.PURPOSE. The owner is CL for client funds or CO for company. The holder says where the money is or whom it concerns: a partner code such as ALI, JETON or AQN (a PARTNER_CONFIG, so always within one project), OWNDESK for the own-desk configuration of the reseller project, OWN for NPL's own wallets, a receiver, a sender, a markup-share party, PROJECT for the project as a whole, or NONE for income, expense and clearing accounts. One account exists per combination actually used; accounts are created on first posting (LEDGER_ACCOUNT: code, ownership, holder_type, holder_id, currency, purpose, kind).

Debit-normal accounts (assets, expenses) grow with debits; credit-normal accounts (liabilities, income) grow with credits. A variance account with a credit balance is a gain. ADVANCE does not exist because NPL never advances money to a receiver. The former single VARIANCE purpose is replaced by the four VAR_ accounts; the former referral payable is SHARE_PAYABLE.

5 · Reading a posting table

Each worked example shows one transaction as a table. Debit and Credit are the amounts in bookkeeping's two columns; In / Out says the same thing in one word: every transaction moves value from somewhere to somewhere, a debit marks the account the value goes to (IN) and a credit the account it comes from (OUT). The word after the dot says what kind of account it is. money is a place where money sits (a partner, a wallet, a balance): IN means money arrives there, OUT that it leaves. promise is what NPL has undertaken to deliver: OUT means NPL now holds money on the strength of a promise (the promise is the source), IN means the promise is being fulfilled. earned is NPL's margin or income: OUT means earnings are the source of money that lands somewhere, usually NPL's pool at a partner. cost is what NPL bears: IN means value goes to a cost. A money or cost account is full when more has gone in than out; a promise or earned account is full when more has come out of it than gone in, because those two are sources by nature; the balance tables show the right figure either way. A reader who ignores the Debit and Credit columns and reads only Account, In / Out and Why will follow every example. Owner is the ownership tag; Cost component is the breakdown tag; Why says in plain words what that line records. The running example is project Evo: Sender A, Receiver X (receiver group A, the default group) with entities Entity X1 and Entity X2; partner Ali (fee_in_rate: its cost is inside its rate), partner Jeton (fee_on_market + 1.25 %, Evo's Jeton fee) and partner Aquanow (fee_on_market + 0.80 %, rebate 0.3 % of the amount). Rates: market 0.9000 EUR per USDT, Ali 0.8910 (Ali's cost is assumed at 1 % inside its rate; the real figure changes daily). Fee structure: rate_basis market (the sender is priced on the market rate and Ali converts at its own), fixed, 2.00 % in total: this sender type pays 1.40 % (Evo's other type pays 1.00 %) and the receiver always pays 0.60 %. The arithmetic is in the Calculation Specification; the headline figures for deal 1 are:

8 · Pattern C — settlement, confirmation, bank fees, shortfalls, rounding

A settlement drains a partner's holding into one or many lines; each line is confirmed by its receiver in full or short. The bank-fee treatment of the project or of a FEE_DECISION decides who bears a fee the bank took.

12 · Pattern G — inter-group offsets

An offset is not an account. 18 · Pattern M — the reseller project (NPL-GR): two invoices, one remittance deal

NPL-GR, a business unit of NPL, buys from vendors at a preferential price and sells to its customers at a retail price. There are two invoices per deal: the vendor's invoice to NPL-GR, and NPL-GR's resale invoice to its customer, both in the vendor's currency. The difference is the game reseller revenue, and it must stay intact whatever happens afterwards. The customer pays the resale invoice through the remittance service with NPL's remittance markup on top; that markup pays for the remittance work, covers the partner's cost and absorbs the forex result of paying the vendor in instalments at different days' rates, and what is left is remittance earnings (NPL, 10 October).

The ledger therefore keeps three things apart. The reseller books are two postings per deal in the invoice currency, one per invoice, plus a claim on the remittance side. The remittance deal posts exactly like any client project, with NPL-GR as the client (holder PROJECT = the NPL-GR remittance project) and the resale invoice as the obligation the deal group pays. The forex result of each discharging line is a true-up between NPL-GR's USDT balance and NPL's pool, booked to VAR_FX_TIMING. The example uses NPL's own figures: a 9,000 EUR vendor invoice resold at 9,450 EUR, collected in THB, paid to the vendor in USDT in two instalments, the first at the group's invoice_rate (1.148369 USDT per EUR, 0.8708 the other way) and the second at 1.142505 (0.87527). Both payout routes are shown: Ali pays the first instalment; the balance hops to NPL's own desk, which pays the second and the margin line.

19 · Pattern N — earnings kept in the unconverted balance and offset between partners

Partners convert everything they receive, so NPL's margin is converted along with the client's money and rests at the partner in the payout currency, invisible to the partner (EARNINGS_RECEIVABLE, Pattern A). NPL's practice (10 October) is to take its earnings out of collections that are not yet converted instead, and to offset between partners: where Jeton has converted a large collection in full, and Ali holds a collection that converts tomorrow, NPL leaves in Ali's USDT balance the USDT equivalent of its margin at Jeton and of its margin on the Ali deal, and converts only the rest. Jeton's EUR balance is then entirely the client's; at Ali the USDT balance is NPL's and the EUR balance the client's.

In the ledger this is two things. Retention is a conversion that keeps the deal's own margin in the collection currency (CONVERSION.residual_in), so EARN_GROSS is recognised in USDT rather than EUR. The offset is an exchange at the day's rate: NPL's EUR at one partner becomes client money there, the same value of the client's unconverted USDT at another partner becomes NPL's, and the client's claim is re-denominated accordingly. For the client it is a conversion NPL performed out of its own earnings; for NPL it is a currency swap, recorded in FX_RESULT. Neither moves money physically, and neither changes any client's entitlement.

19a · Pattern O — reroute after conversion

19b · Pattern P — interruptions between instruction and recording

The principle first: no background job ever posts money. A job may stamp a clock, expire a quote, attach evidence or raise an alert, but every posting is made by a controlled action of a person, or by the engine as part of one (Architecture note, Section 4). A failure between an instruction to a partner and its recording can therefore delay a posting; it cannot duplicate one, because the action that posts carries an idempotency key and is refused the second time (rule 1a, enforced by the unique index and the single database transaction of rule 1b; the instruction is committed before the partner is called or told, so a failure after the partner acted leaves a reconcilable instruction, never an orphan). Three cases follow from that principle; their worked postings are written after Phase 1, when the behaviour can be exercised on staging (Phase 2, M7).

Granularity, and the three sums that must agree. An opening entry is never an aggregate per partner and currency. The month-to-date tier volume of the opening month is opened as a counter line of kind month_volume, not as a posting (Calculation Specification 5a) (Test Strategy G-OPEN).

23 · Who sees what, and who may post

Rights are rows, not text: ROLE holds the three roles of today, operations, finance and management, and ROLE_RIGHT says per controlled action which role may initiate and which may approve, with the role added on escalation; the approver is never the initiator.

  • Operations (sees_economics false) sees operational fields, sender-facing prices and partner rates as entered, but never a ledger balance, a margin, a pool, a share, a rebate, a cost breakdown or a variance, on any screen, export, message or report.
  • Finance sees all postings and balances, owns reconciliation and the month-end true-ups, initiates adjustments, balance conversions and dues recovery, recommends covered bank fees and fee caps (FEE_DECISION.recommended_by), approves honoured expired rates and quote-validity extensions, and records a partner's hold and its clearance; a screening that is not clear is Management's second look, and no role overrides a reject.
  • Management approves the actions the ledger patterns depend on: approve_loss_split, balance_conversion, recover_dues, earnings_offset (requested by Finance), cross_group_payout, cover_bank_fee, raise_fee_cap, enable_own_desk, settlements above limit and past the cutoff, own-wallet outbound transfers, and post-money voids. Every controlled action is audited with actor, time, reason and record version.
