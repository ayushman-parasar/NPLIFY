Status: Built against ERD & Data Model Draft v5.2, the Transaction Lifecycle & Approval State Machines v1.1 and the Project Understanding v1.1 (§9, roles and visibility). It turns the one-sentence rule — Operations sees the least, Finance sees everything Operations sees plus the economics, Management sees everything — into a concrete table: for every module and every group of fields, who may view, enter, propose and approve. Appendix A applies the same classification to every one of the 430 fields of the 58 tables and views, so the build has one list to implement and the acceptance tests in Section 6 have one list to check against: 90 fields are never shown to Operations.
Audience: NPL Management, who own the roles, and the New XP engineering team, for whom this is the build and acceptance reference for permissions.

1 · The model in one page

Three roles, three nested rings. Operations runs deals: quotes, verifies collections, instructs conversions, prepares disbursements, enters partner rates, requests reroutes and records partner approvals. Finance sees everything Operations sees plus the economics, approves what Operations initiates, resolves exceptions, proposes configuration and owns reconciliation. Management sees everything and approves the heavier actions, every configuration change and every user change. Visibility is cumulative — a wider role never loses a narrower role's view — and approval is maker-checker: the person who initiates an action is never the person who approves it, enforced by the server, not by convention.

Derived economics — the eight things Operations never sees. The reference rate at capture · the spread and rolling averages · NPL's margin and earnings · the pool resting at a partner · the cost breakdown (partner cost, bank and network fees, variance, referral, rounding) · ledger balances · variances and exceptions in figures · referral and rebate accruals. The rule applies on any screen, export, message or report; it is enforced where values leave the system, not in the user interface.

2 · Field groups

Every field of every table belongs to exactly one of eight groups, and the group decides who sees it. The groups are the unit of enforcement: the build filters by group, and the tests check by group.

5 · Enforcement rules (build reference)

  1. Role on the user. USER.role is one of operations, finance, management; one role per user; rights follow Sections 3 and 4 with no per-user exceptions.
  2. Server-side field filtering on every read. Each table's fields carry the group of Appendix A. Any read by an Operations user strips the fields of groups Reference rate and comparison, Margin, pool, cost breakdown and Ledger and balances before the response is built; the same filter applies to list views, search, exports, generated documents, notifications, messages and scheduled reports. The user interface receives only what the role may see, so nothing can be revealed by inspecting a page or a cached response.
  3. Derived figures follow their inputs. A figure computed from a hidden field is hidden; a total, average or chart that would reveal a hidden field is hidden. The one exception is deliberate: Operations sees the amount available to pay out per partner × currency as a single number, because preparing a disbursement needs it.
  4. Writes are role-scoped. Operations may create and edit operational records only (group Operational, Sender-facing prices through quoting, Partner rates as entered); Finance may additionally resolve exceptions and record reconciliation; configuration and users are written only through an approved proposal.
  5. Maker-checker at the approval endpoint. APPROVAL.approved_by must differ from APPROVAL.requested_by; Operations is never an approver; the escalation conditions of Section 4 add a Management approval rather than replacing Finance's; when only one Finance user is on shift a Management countersignature stands in for the second Finance user and is recorded as a countersignature.
  6. Nothing is deleted. Rejections keep the request, the decision and the mandatory reason; a re-submission is a new approval that points at the rejected one; AUDIT_LOG records actor, time, reason and record version for every controlled action and every configuration change.
  7. Users and roles need two Management users. Creating, changing or removing a user or role is a Management action approved by a second Management user.
  8. Exceptions are shown as blockers, not as figures. A frozen partner × pair, an open variance exception, an open offset and custody aging appear to Operations as the state that blocks their work and the role that must act; the spread, variance, offset amount and custody balance are Finance's view.
One point for Management to confirm. Operations enters the partner's rate and sees the sender rate and the amount the receiver is owed. For a disclosed-rate partner those three numbers let a careful user work out NPL's gross margin on a deal by hand. The policy hides the computed figures, not the inputs Operations must handle; this follows today's practice (Operations types the agent rate) and the Understanding document's wording. If Management wants the inference closed too, the partner's rate would have to be entered by Finance — a change to who does what, not to this matrix.

6 · Acceptance tests

The build passes when every test below holds for every role. Tests are stated as scenario → expected outcome; the field lists they refer to are those of Appendix A.

TestScenarioExpected
Field stripping on readAn Operations user requests any deal, conversion, balance conversion or reroute through the API or the screen.The response contains no field of groups REF, ECON or LEDGER (Appendix A lists them by table); the same request by Finance contains them all.
Field stripping on exportOperations exports a deal list, a settlement report or a quote package to CSV, PDF or a message.No column, footnote or total derived from groups REF, ECON or LEDGER appears; Finance's export of the same report carries them.

Maker-checkerA user approves an APPROVAL they requested themselves, or Operations approves anything.The server refuses with a reason; nothing changes; the attempt is in the audit log.
Escalation adds, never replacesA disbursement above the settlement limit is approved by Finance only.It stays Approved-pending-Management; release is refused until a Management approval exists in addition to Finance's.
