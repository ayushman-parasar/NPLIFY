1 · Conventions

  • Rates are stored normalised to the pair's quote_direction (out units per 1 in unit unless the pair says in_per_out) at CURRENCY_PAIR.rate_precision. A partner's rate is stored as quoted (PARTNER_RATE_VERSION.quoted_rate, quoted_direction) and the normalised rate is derived: equal to the quote when the directions agree, 1 ÷ quote at the pair's precision when they differ.
  • Amounts are exact decimals; fiat and USDT amounts are computed to 2 decimals (BTC-like assets to 8) with round half up, then rounded as the pair's option says (§9). Every intermediate figure in this document is computed, not typed.
  • Percentages are stored as fractions (1.4 % = 0.014). sender_pays_pct + receiver_pays_pct = FEE_STRUCTURE.pct.
  • Precision of a check: an acceptance vector matches when the computed figure and NPL's figure agree to the cent, or to the unit where NPL's sheet rounds to the unit.

2 · Rate basis, derived rates and the breach check

FEE_STRUCTURE.rate_basis says which rate the sender is priced on: market (the fee is disclosed; the partner's cost comes out of NPL's fee parts — Evo, the reseller projects) or agent_rate (a calculated rate, breakdown not disclosed; NPL keeps the whole fee and the partner's cost sits on top — BF via Ali). The partner's own pricing is PARTNER_PAIR.partner_pricing: fee_in_rate (its cost is inside its rate, never posted; Ali, assumed 1 %) or fee_on_market (it converts at market and states partner_fee_pct separately; Jeton 1.25 %, Aquanow 0.80 %).

basis_rate = market_rate if rate_basis = market = partner_rate_version.rate if rate_basis = agent_rate rate = quoted_rate if quoted_direction = pair.quote_direction = round(1 ÷ quoted_rate, rate_precision) otherwise spread = 1 − partner_rate ÷ market_rate (what the partner keeps against market, fee_in_rate) breach = spread > avg_2w_spread × (1 + variance_threshold_rel) → the pair freezes until Finance decides

2a · When the breach check cannot be trusted

The breach check of Section 2 compares today's spread with the average spread of the previous two weeks. Three situations make that comparison unreliable; each has its own rule. The three values the rules use are project thresholds set by NPL Finance (THRESHOLD kinds rate_min_observations, rate_fallback_spread_abs and rate_abs_tolerance); the figures below are illustrative. Stale rates, the freeze on a breached pair and the use of confirmed versions only are unchanged.

SituationRuleWhat the system does
Too little historyWith fewer than rate_min_observations confirmed rate versions in the two-week window (a new partner, a new pair, a long holiday), the two-week average is not used.The spread is compared with the Management-approved fallback threshold instead: breach = spread > rate_fallback_spread_abs. Finance confirms the version with a reason; the pair is not frozen merely for lack of history.
Unusable baselineWhen avg_2w_spread is zero or negative (the partner has been pricing at or better than market), the percentage formula has no meaning.The percentage formula is not run. The fallback threshold applies as above, and the version is flagged for Finance to confirm.
Unusual rate, favourable or notA spread outside an absolute tolerance around the two-week average, in either direction: spread < avg_2w_spread − rate_abs_tolerance or spread > avg_2w_spread + rate_abs_tolerance.The version is flagged for Finance to verify, not rejected and not frozen: an unusually favourable rate is checked (direction slip, wrong pair, stale partner) before it is used. The existing breach rule still applies on top for an unfavourable spread.
observations = count of confirmed PARTNER_RATE_VERSION rows for the partner × pair in the last 14 days if observations < rate_min_observations or avg_2w_spread ≤ 0: breach = spread > rate_fallback_spread_abs (fallback; no percentage formula) else: breach = spread > avg_2w_spread × (1 + variance_threshold_rel) (Section 2, unchanged) flag_for_verification = |spread − avg_2w_spread| > rate_abs_tolerance (either direction; Finance confirms, nothing freezes)
Typical Ali spread 1.00 %, variance_threshold_rel 20 %: a spread above 1.20 % breaches as today. New partner with four confirmed versions and rate_min_observations 10: the average is ignored and a spread above the fallback 1.50 % breaches. A day on which Ali quotes 0.30 % below market (spread −0.30 %) with rate_abs_tolerance 0.50 %: no breach, but the version is flagged and Finance verifies the direction and the pair before it is used. The three rules are tested in Phase 1 (M1) with one case each.

3 · Fee parts on the principal

The sender's part and the receiver's part are each a percentage of the principal at the basis rate. They are never compounded on each other (300,000 USDT × 0.9000 − 2 % = 264,600).

principal = amount_in × basis_rate (fixed_in) fee_sender_part = principal × sender_pays_pct fee_receiver_part = principal × receiver_pays_pct sender_rate = basis_rate × (1 − sender_pays_pct) (the rate the sender is given) gross_out = amount_in × sender_rate = principal − fee_sender_part client_net = gross_out − fee_receiver_part = principal × (1 − sender_pays_pct − receiver_pays_pct)
  • 10,000 USDT × market 0.9000 = 9,000.00 EUR (the principal at the basis rate)
  • sender part = 9,000 × 1.40 % = 126.00; receiver part = 9,000 × 0.60 % = 54.00
  • sender rate = 0.9000 × (1 − 1.40 %) = 0.88740; gross out = 10,000 × 0.88740 = 8,874.00
  • client net = 9,000 × (1 − 2.00 %) = 8,820.00 = gross out − receiver part
Confirmed rule (NPL, 11 October). Both parts are on the principal. NPL's fee sheet for Evo prints the receiver side as "EUR 25,704 − 0.6 % = 25,550", which takes the receiver's part from the gross out; on the principal it is 25,547.88, a difference of 0.6 % × 1.4 % of the principal (about 0.008 %). The sheet's receiver line is to be brought in line with the rule; the engine follows the rule.

4 · Fixed and variable fee modes; the minimum-margin check

In fixed mode pct is applied as above. In variable mode the fee is whatever leaves NPL its minimum margin after the partner's cost, within a floor and a cap: this is the check that decides whether 2 % is enough when the agent rate is high.

required_pct = min_margin_pct + spread if rate_basis = market (the partner's cost comes out of the fee) = min_margin_pct if rate_basis = agent_rate fee_pct = min(max_markup_pct, max(min_markup_pct, required_pct)) below_margin = fee_pct < required_pct → warn, never refuse a quote; Finance may ask the partner for a better rate or recommend a fee above the cap (raise_fee_cap, Management approves)

5 · Fee tiers by monthly volume

tier = the FEE_TIER of the structure with the highest min_monthly_volume ≤ PROJECT_MONTH_VOLUME at quote portion_1 = max(0, threshold_of_next_tier − PROJECT_MONTH_VOLUME) capped at amount_in, priced at tier.pct portion_2 = amount_in − portion_1 priced at next_tier.pct fee_parts = Σ portions × basis_rate × pct (sender and receiver parts in the structure's proportion) — the blended sum

GDC: 2.5 % up to USD 500,000 a month, 2.25 % above; a leg that takes the month past the threshold is split into two DEAL_FEE_TIER portions.

5a · The month-volume counter and what a void does to it

PROJECT_MONTH_VOLUME(project, month, ccy) = Σ amount_in × basis_rate of forward legs priced in the calendar month, in FEE_TIER.volume_currency, excluding legs voided before or after money moved tier at quote = the tier with the highest min_monthly_volume ≤ PROJECT_MONTH_VOLUME at the moment of pricing

A void lowers the counter for every quote priced after it; it never re-prices a leg already priced (pricing fields are write-once), so a leg that was split across two tiers keeps its split even if an earlier deal in the month is later voided. A return leg and a balance conversion do not count. Two quotes priced at the same moment read the counter under the lock on the month-volume row, so they are ordered and the second sees the first (the one-line database rule of Phase 1, M0 and M3); this is tested with real GDC data rather than designed for every edge case now. A backdated correction is a new leg priced today and counts in today's month.

6 · Amount modes: fixed_in and target_out

A collect-first deal prices what arrived (fixed_in, §3). A quote-first deal for a given out amount (target_out: an invoice, a GDC USD amount, a reseller's EUR) applies the fee as a markup on the target, which is how NPL's sheets print it (EUR 50,000 × 1.01 ÷ rate; USD 84,482 + 2.5 %).

amount_in = target_out × (1 + sender_pays_pct) ÷ basis_rate (then cash-rounded if collected in cash, §9) sender_rate = basis_rate ÷ (1 + sender_pays_pct) (printed as target ÷ amount_in) client_net = target_out × (1 − receiver_pays_pct) + cash_rounding_surplus fee_sender_part = amount_in × basis_rate − (target_out + cash_rounding_surplus)

Note: × (1 + s) and ÷ (1 − s) differ by s² (0.01 % at a 1 % fee). NPL's convention, × (1 + s), is adopted; the fixed_in formula keeps × (1 − s). The cash-rounding surplus is the client's (in NPL-GR, NPL-GR's).

  • NPL-GR: invoice 9,450 EUR ÷ 0.8708 = 10,852.09 USDT to deliver
  • amount_in (exact) = 10,852.09 × (1 + 2 %) × 32.50 THB per USDT = 359,746.78 THB; collected as cash, rounded up to the 1,000 = 360,000 THB
  • client net = 360,000 ÷ 32.50 × (1 − 2 %)… no: in target mode the fee is on the target, so client net = target + the cash-rounding surplus = 10,859.73 USDT (7.63 surplus); sender part = amount_in × market − client net = 217.19
  • Ali converts at 1 ÷ 32.83: delivered 10,966.15; NPL earnings = 10,966.15 − 10,859.73 = 106.42 = the 2 % markup less Ali's 1 %

7 · Customer-facing figures

customer_facing_fee_pct = sender_pays_pct + receiver_pays_pct if customer_facing_fee_basis = actual = sender_pays_pct on the agent rate if sender_pays_on_agent_rate = FEE_STRUCTURE.customer_facing_fee_pct if fixed (BF via Ali prints 1 %) customer_facing_rate = sender_rate ÷ (1 − customer_facing_fee_pct) — the "market rate" the quote prints, derived, never typed

8 · What the partner delivers, and NPL's earnings

fee_in_rate: delivered = amount_in × partner_rate; partner_cost (inferred) = amount_in × (market − partner_rate), information only fee_on_market: converted = amount_in × market_rate; partner_cost = converted × partner_fee_pct; delivered = converted − partner_cost earnings = delivered − client_net (NPL's margin: EARN_GROSS − EXP_PARTNER) EARN_GROSS = converted − client_net (fee_on_market: the full fee parts, the partner's cost booked apart as EXP_PARTNER; fee_in_rate: the fee parts less the partner's hidden cost — equal to earnings) retained_in = earnings_in_out_currency ÷ partner_rate when the margin is kept in the collection currency (CONVERSION.residual_in) converted_in = amount_in − retained_in − offset_in (what the partner actually converts, §13)
  • Ali (fee_in_rate) converts at 0.8910: delivered = 10,000 × 0.8910 = 8,910.00; NPL earnings = 8,910.00 − 8,820.00 = 90.00; Ali's inferred cost = 10,000 × (0.9000 − 0.8910) = 90.00, information only
  • Jeton (fee_on_market 1.25 %) converts at market: 9,000.00; partner cost = 9,000.00 × 1.25 % = 112.50; delivered 8,887.50; fee charged 180.00 (EARN_GROSS); NPL earnings = 8,887.50 − 8,820.00 = 67.50

9 · Rounding and precision

payout: amount_rounding = exact | truncate_unit | nearest_unit | round_up_unit | nearest_n | round_up_n (rounding_unit) payable = round(client_net, option); residual = client_net − payable (signed) residual > 0 → stays client money in DUE; residual < 0 → NPL's cost, EXP_ROUNDING, out of its margin cash in: cash_rounding = truncate_n | nearest_n | round_up_n (cash_rounding_unit: AED 100, USD cash 10, THB 1,000) — the collection side; a cash payout rounds by the pair's amount_rounding (round_up_n 1,000 for THB hand-overs) amount_in = round(exact amount_in, option); surplus = (amount_in − exact) × basis_rate × (1 − fee) → the client's rates: rate_precision per pair (6 for EUR/USDT, 8 for BTC); intermediate amounts 2 dp (8 for BTC), round half up

Bank transfers go out exact, cents included; rounding to a unit applies where the pair says so, in practice to cash hand-overs (NPL, 11 October). USDT paid by reseller customers is rounded up.

  • THB payout in cash: client net 95,844.00 THB, cash_rounding round_up_n 1,000 → 96,000.00; residual = 95,844.00 − 96,000.00 = -156.00 (negative: NPL's cost, EXP_ROUNDING); NPL's pool gets 966.00 -156.00 = 810.00

12 · Variances, by cause

expected_out = converted_in × partner_rate_version.rate (as priced) actual_out = what the partner booked (partner_calculates) or expected_out (npl_calculates) variance = expected_out − actual_out (+ = NPL received less than priced) client_net unchanged (write-once); pool = actual_out − client_net; the variance posts to VAR_CONVERSION partner_calculates differences and basis variance on a reroute VAR_CUTOFF rate stamped at lock vs rate used after the partner's cutoff (conversion_due_date) VAR_RATE_HONOUR an expired quote's rate honoured by override vs the day's rate VAR_FX_TIMING an obligation priced on one day and discharged on another (§14)
  • partner_calculates: 3,000 USDT expected at 0.8910 = 2,673.00, booked at 0.8905 = 2,671.50; variance = 1.50 → VAR_CONVERSION; client net 2,646.00 unchanged
  • cutoff timing: locked 0.8910, converted next day at 0.8890 → 6.00 → VAR_CUTOFF
  • honoured expired rate: 0.8910 honoured, Ali at 0.8860 → 15.00 → VAR_RATE_HONOUR

retained_in = earnings ÷ partner_rate (this deal's margin kept unconverted, residual_in) offset_to = from_amount ÷ to_partner_rate_of_day (EARNINGS_OFFSET: NPL's margin at the from-partner, in USDT at the to-partner) converted_in = amount_in − retained_in − offset_to client_out = converted_in × partner_rate = client_net − from_amount already delivered at the from-partner neutral for the client: Σ client entitlement unchanged; NPL's EARN_GROSS for the deal is recognised in the collection currency

19a · Machine-readable vectors and the regression gate

Vector sets A (L1 to L13) and B (B1 to B23, numbered as in the Reading Companion's identifier appendix) are published with this document as one machine-readable file (vectors-v5.9.csv: one row per vector with every input and every expected figure), which is the file the automated tests read. Every release runs the full vector set together with the ledger fixtures (the 61 worked transactions), the state-machine tests and the visibility tests; a failing exact test blocks the release. The gate is stated in the Test Strategy & Coverage document and continues under the support bundle after go-live; the first automated runs are in Phase 1 (M4).

19b · Identifiers of the vector B rows

The rows of Section 19 are numbered B1 to B23 in the order printed, so that the test suite, the Reading Companion's identifier register and NPL's sheet can refer to one row by one name. The numbers are frozen: a row is never renumbered and a number is never reused; a new case takes the next number (B24).

19c · Which cases the two vector sets cover

A vector is one worked deal: its inputs (amount, rates, fee parts) and the figure the system must produce. Set A (L1 to L13, Section 18 and 18a) are examples New XP wrote and worked through the pricing formula by hand; they prove the formula does what this specification says, not that NPL agrees with the result. Set B (B1 to B23, Section 19) are NPL's own numbers: each row takes a line from NPL's fee sheet of 8 October (corrected 21 October) or a figure NPL supplied on 21 October for the cases the sheet had no line for, recomputes it with the formula and shows whether the two agree. A matching B row means the system reproduces what NPL charges today, which is the acceptance test NPL cares about; a matching A row means the system matches our reading of the rules.

20 · Acceptance

  1. The engine reproduces every vector of §18 and §19 exactly (to the cent, or to the unit where the sheet rounds), and every posting amount of the Ledger Posting Design.
  2. Fee parts are computed on the principal (§3) and never compounded; a target-out quote applies the fee as a markup on the target (§6).
  3. Every rounding step uses the pair's configured option and records the signed residual with its owner (§9).
  4. Variances are posted to the account of their cause (§12); the forex true-up of each instalment is posted per line (§14); an earnings offset leaves every client entitlement unchanged (§13).
  5. NPL_PROFIT returns remittance earnings, reseller earnings and their sum, per currency, and the same figures through REPORTING_VALUE (§15, §17).

Vectors A are worked examples; vectors B are NPL's figures from the fee table of 8 October 2026.
