1 · The model in one page

Three roles, three nested rings. Operations runs deals: quotes and extends quotes, screens wallets, verifies collections, instructs conversions, prepares settlements, enters partner rates as quoted, requests reroutes, records destination approvals and loss events. Finance sees everything Operations sees plus the economics, approves what Operations initiates, resolves exceptions, recommends covered fees, requests balance conversions, dues recovery, earnings offsets and loss splits, proposes configuration and owns reconciliation. Management sees everything and approves the heavier actions, every configuration change and every user change. Visibility is cumulative — a wider role never loses a narrower role's view — and approval is maker-checker: the person who initiates an action is never the person who approves it, enforced by the server from the ROLE_RIGHT rows, not by convention.

Derived economics — what Operations never sees. The reference rate at capture · the spread and rolling averages · NPL's margin and earnings, retained or offset · the pool resting at a partner · the cost breakdown (partner fee, bank and network fees, rounding, loss) · the four variances by cause and FX_RESULT · ledger balances · markup-share and rebate accruals · a loss event's split, funding and recoveries · the reseller and remittance margins and NPL's profit · any figure converted into the reporting currency. The rule applies on any screen, export, message or report; it is enforced where values leave the system, not in the user interface. Operations does see the customer-facing fee and rate a quote prints: they are what the sender sees.

5 · Enforcement rules (build reference)

  1. Role on the user, rights on the role. USER.role_id names one ROLE (operations, finance, management today); ROLE.sees_economics is false for Operations; ROLE_RIGHT holds one row per role × controlled action with may_initiate, may_approve and the role added on escalation. Rights follow Sections 3 and 4 with no per-user exceptions; a new role is a Management-approved configuration change.
  2. Server-side field filtering on every read. Each table's fields carry the group of Appendix A. Any read by a role whose sees_economics is false strips the fields of groups Reference rate and comparison, Margin, pool, cost breakdown and Ledger and balances before the response is built; the same filter applies to list views, search, exports, generated documents, notifications, messages, the client journal and scheduled reports. The user interface receives only what the role may see, so nothing can be revealed by inspecting a page or a cached response.
  3. Derived figures follow their inputs. A figure computed from a hidden field is hidden; a total, average, chart or reporting-currency value that would reveal a hidden field is hidden. The one exception is deliberate: Operations sees the amount available to pay out per partner × currency as a single number, because preparing a settlement needs it. Customer-facing figures are not derived economics: they are what the quote prints.
  4. Writes are role-scoped. Operations may create and edit operational records only (group Operational, Sender-facing prices through quoting, Partner rates as entered); Finance may additionally resolve exceptions, record reconciliation, recoveries and true-ups; configuration, system settings, users and roles are written only through an approved proposal.
  5. Maker-checker at the approval endpoint. APPROVAL.approved_by must differ from APPROVAL.requested_by; the approver's role must hold may_approve for the action in ROLE_RIGHT; the escalation conditions of Section 4 add a Management approval rather than replacing Finance's; when only one Finance user is on shift a Management countersignature stands in for the second Finance user and is recorded as a countersignature.
  6. Nothing is deleted. Rejections keep the request, the decision and the mandatory reason; a re-submission is a new approval that points at the rejected one; AUDIT_LOG records actor, time, reason and record version for every controlled action and every configuration change.
  7. Users and roles need two Management users. Creating, changing or removing a user, a role or a ROLE_RIGHT row is a Management action approved by a second Management user.
  8. Exceptions are shown as blockers, not as figures. A frozen partner × pair, an open variance exception, an open offset, custody aging, a negative pool, an unfunded make-good and an open loss recovery appear to Operations as the state that blocks their work and the role that must act; the spread, variance, offset amount, pool and loss shares are Finance's view.
  9. Messages print only what the template allows. MESSAGE_TEMPLATE.printed_fields is the list of model fields a template may render; a quote template is sender-facing only, so a message can never carry a partner rate, a margin or a cost whichever role sends it.
  10. Bank account details are read in full and read-logged. BANK_ACCOUNT_DETAILS belongs to group Configuration and master data: Operations enters a row from the rail's template, Finance or Management approves it (controlled action bank_details_approve), and all three roles read every field unmasked, on one card component, because the people who instruct and verify transfers need the details as the bank sees them. In return every full read — card, export or API — writes an AUDIT_LOG row with the actor and time, and the quarterly access review (5a) samples those rows. The evidence files (bank letters, KYC, legal documents) stay under the restricted prefix of the file store, which only Finance and Management resolve.
  11. Screening outcomes have three owners, and there is no override right. (1) A partner's sanctions or AML hold, freeze or decline is the partner's decision: no role at NPL has a right that lifts it; NPL records it as the event it is (REROUTE with trigger compliance_hold, LOSS_EVENT with reason account_frozen, EMERGENCY_ACTION of kind destination_freeze), routes around it, and records the partner's clearance reference when the partner lifts it. (2) NPL's own wallet screening is operational when the provider's report is clear under its Strict rulebook (Operations records accept) and a Management decision otherwise: the screening row requests the second look, Management decides accept, accept_alternate or reject with a reason (ROLE_RIGHT screening_second_look: may_initiate Operations, may_approve Management; no Finance row), and the approver is never the person who screened. The Finance right override_screening_reject no longer exists. (3) A technical false positive is a new screening row with its evidence, decided on its own report. Enforcement: WALLET_SCREENING.decision is write-once for every role and every channel (screen, API, export re-import, a re-submitted or replayed approval request, which carries the same idempotency key and returns the first result without writing); the provider's report is operational data the three roles read, while a partner's clearance letter is an evidence file under the restricted prefix.
  12. The field groups apply outside the application too. The fallback workbook of the Architecture (the export every five minutes) is two workbooks, not two tabs: an operational workbook (open deals and their states, cutoffs, pending approvals, the partner rates of the day as entered, the amount available to pay out per partner and currency) shared with the Operations, Finance and Management accounts, and a Finance workbook (partner balances, pool, margins, spreads, the reconciliation figures) shared with Finance and Management only; tab-level protection is not relied on. Both are shared to named accounts, never by link; the export job writes through a service account; a leaver's account is removed from both on the day (procedure A.10) and the share lists are compared with the user list at the quarterly access review. Generated extracts and scheduled reports pass the same server-side filter as a screen (rule 2). Notifications, monitoring alarms, chat messages and incidents raised on the delivery board name the item and the state in operational terms (rule 8) and carry no figure of the groups Reference rate and comparison, Margin, pool, cost breakdown or Ledger and balances; the alarms of the Architecture's monitoring carry no deal figures at all.
  13. Email OTP settings are protected master data, and a code is never readable. The project's Email OTP switch and, on each sender, its one registered approver address and its registered chat group (platform, group name, group id; every sender has its own group) belong to group Configuration and master data and change only through the controlled action set_email_otp_settings: Operations enters, Finance approves, never the same person, every change an APPROVAL and an AUDIT_LOG row with before and after. A new approver address is active only after a verification code sent to it comes back through the registered chat group and is entered; a chat-group change carries a screenshot from the group as evidence. While a change is pending the server refuses to send an approval email for that sender, and it never sends one to any address but the one stored on the sender; a sender with no approved address cannot be quoted while the switch is on; the address is not on the quote screen. A change of address voids that sender's open codes, so a live code never moves. The one-time code exists only in the email and in memory at generation: the database holds its hash, which is in no field group and on no screen, export or API for any role; the approval row shows the code reference, the status, the stamps and the evidence. The senders' approver addresses are listed, per project, on the quarterly access review (5a) for Management to confirm. Inbound mail is the customer's only when it comes from the registered address, and never approves (Lifecycle & Approvals 2e; Configuration Schema rule 16).
One point for Management to confirm. Operations enters the partner's rate and sees the sender rate and the amount the receiver is owed. For a fee_on_market partner those three numbers let a careful user work out NPL's gross margin on a deal by hand. The policy hides the computed figures, not the inputs Operations must handle; this follows today's practice (Operations types the agent rate). If Management wants the inference closed too, the partner's rate would have to be entered by Finance — a change to who does what, not to this matrix.

5a · Absence cover, scoped rights and delegation

Three questions from the design review, answered with NPL's decisions of 19 and 20 October. None of them adds a role or a per-user right; rule 1 of Section 5 stands.

14. Cover for staff absence is the role, not a delegation

Rights belong to the role, so any user holding a role may do what that role permits; nothing is handed from one person to another when someone is on leave, off shift or unreachable. If a whole role is unavailable, a user of a wider role may work a narrower role's steps through an account of that role (a Finance user takes an Operations-role account for the Operations steps and returns to the Finance account for the Finance steps). One safeguard makes this safe: the “approver is not the initiator” check of rule 5 is made on the person, not the account. Every USER row names the natural person behind it (USER.person_ref, ERD Appendix B.5), the two accounts of one person carry the same person_ref, and APPROVAL refuses an approver whose person_ref equals the initiator's. The Management countersignature when only one Finance user is on shift (rule 5) is unchanged.

Scoped rights: documented, not built

A per-user, per-project scope (a user limited to named projects, approved by Management) is a possible later addition for an unusually sensitive counterparty. It is recorded here as an option and is not built unless NPL chooses it; until then there are no per-user exceptions and visibility is by role only.

Time-limited delegation: not in P0

The reviewer's revised recommendation is adopted as written: “Define backup approval arrangements for staff absence, holidays and emergencies, ensuring maker-checker controls remain intact. Automated temporary delegation can be considered in a later phase.” Rule 14 is that arrangement. No delegation feature, no expiring rights and no delegation records exist in the P0 model.

Periodic access review

Management reviews users, roles, dormant accounts and leavers once a quarter and records the review; the procedure is A.10 of the Client Operating Procedures, and the users-and-access report that supports it is built in Phase 2 (M6).
