NPLify · P0 Technical Baseline

Cutover Approach

New XP Technologies Limited

22 October 2026 · Confidential

NPL runs on spreadsheets today. The plan is to start using NPLify on a staging instance: after every change to a spreadsheet the team makes the same change in the app, for three to four weeks, to see that things go in the right direction; earlier deals are entered as well so the history is in one place and the ledger's balances are built from real entries; when everything is right the database is copied to production and the production app is used from a quiet day, chosen a couple of days before. This document gives that plan its steps, its checks and its sign-off.

How to read this pack

Four map pages are now part of the pack as documents of their own, and are the place to start: the Top-Down Map (from the business, through the data model and the deal states, to the scenarios and the reading order), the Deal Flow on one page (Quote, Collect, Convert, Settle, with the roles and the money path), the Workflow Tree (8 classes, 27 sub-classes, 92 procedures, each naming who initiates, who approves, the codes it is written under and the records it touches) and the Workflow Tree on one page. Read them in that order, then this document. Every worked transaction (T), acceptance vector (L, B), walkthrough (W, X), flow (F) and procedure (C, A, D) has one identifier, listed in the Reading Companion's identifier register; the Test Strategy & Coverage document says which test proves each procedure.

1 · The approach in one page

Three things have to be true on the evening of the switch: the configuration in the app describes NPL's business as it is (Configuration Schema), the ledger's balances equal the spreadsheets' balances to the cent, and the team has already run real weeks in the app and trusts it. The plan gets there without touching production until the last day:

  1. Prepare. Load the configuration (clients, projects, partners, pairs, fee structures, parties, accounts, templates, users and roles) into the staging instance; validate it against the schema rules; fix the spreadsheets' inconsistencies that the validation surfaces before going further. Pick the opening date (Section 4).
  2. Run in parallel on staging. For three to four weeks the spreadsheets stay the system of record and the app is run beside them: every change made to a sheet is made in the app the same day, by the same person, through the normal screens and approvals. Finance reconciles every evening (Section 5); every difference goes in the discrepancy log and is explained before the next day starts.
  3. Opening entries on the opening date. On the first day of the parallel run, the opening date chosen by NPL, every balance the sheets show is entered as a verified opening entry: for each project, partner and currency an opening posting against a dedicated opening-balance account, split by sender within each receiver group, with NPL's pool and anything owed to or by a partner as separate lines; Finance verifies each line against the partner statement or wallet balance of that day, Management approves, and the evidence is attached (Section 4). Deals in flight on the opening date are entered by hand as open deals in their current state. Earlier history stays in the archived sheets, read-only; NPLify's reporting starts on the opening date.
  4. Go / no-go. The exit criteria of Section 6 are met or the run is extended; Management signs; NPL names the switch day: the day with the least traffic, a couple of days ahead.
  5. The switch day. Freeze the sheets after the previous day's close; final reconciliation; reset production once, copy the staging database and the file bucket to it, verify, point the users at production. The next day is the first live day; the sheets stay read-only for four weeks as the fallback; the staging instance is removed.
What the parallel run proves, and what it does not. It proves that the configuration prices and routes NPL's real deals the way NPL does, that the postings of each day reproduce the sheets' balances, and that the team can run its day in the app. It does not prove the ledger's arithmetic — that is the Calculation Specification's acceptance vectors and the Ledger Posting Design's worked transactions, which the build already passes — and it does not test every pattern of the Scenario Catalogue: the weeks will bring whichever deals they bring. Patterns that do not occur in the run are exercised once each on staging as scripted test deals before the go/no-go (Section 6).

Scripted test deals never touch a real project. Every real project has a dummy twin project on staging, set up from the same archetype with the same configuration and flagged as a test project (PROJECT.is_test); the scripted deals that exercise patterns the weeks did not bring are entered there. Before staging is copied to production, a verified filter removes every flagged project with all its deals, approvals, evidence and postings; the switch-day checklist (Section 7) confirms zero test rows and that counts and balances exclude the flagged projects. Test users are removed in the same step.

3 · The staging instance and the rules of the parallel run

Staging is a second copy of the production stack in NPL's AWS account (Architecture & Stack Note, Environments): the same image, its own database and bucket, its own sign-in. It exists for the weeks of the run and is removed after go-live. Four rules make the run meaningful:

  1. The sheets lead, the app follows — the same day. Whoever changes a sheet makes the same change in the app before the end of their shift, as the person they are (Operations enters, Finance approves, Management escalates), through the normal screens. No bulk entry, no "Finance types everything": the point is that the roles and approvals are exercised.
  2. Real values, real timestamps. The partner's rate as quoted, the market rate at the time (the app captures its own; the sheet's rate is a check), the real cutoff, the real evidence attached. Where the app's clock would have expired a quote the sheet kept alive, the discrepancy log says so: that is a finding about policy, not an error.
  3. Outside services point at copies. Rates, hash checks and the authenticator app work as in production. The client journal writes to a copy of each client's sheet, never to the client's real sheet, until go-live. No message rendered on staging is sent to a sender or receiver; quote packages are rendered and filed only.
  4. Finance reconciles every evening with the checks of Section 5, before the next day's first deal. A difference is either fixed in the app (an entry missed or mis-entered), fixed in the sheet (the sheet was wrong — these are findings NPL wants), or explained as a known difference of method (rounding at the pair's unit, a fee part the sheet computes differently) and recorded. A difference that cannot be explained stops the run until it is.
Two things the staging weeks are not. They are not training on test data — that happens on the local environment before the run starts, with the Ledger Posting Design's worked transactions as the exercises. And they are not optional for Finance: the opening entries' sign-off and the daily reconciliation are Finance's work, and the go/no-go is signed on Finance's log.

4 · Opening entries

NPL's decision of 20 October: the balances the sheets show on the opening date are entered as verified opening entries, not reconstructed by re-entering months of deals. The ledger stays append-only and every later balance still derives from postings; the opening entries are postings like any other, with evidence attached, and the history before the opening date stays in the archived sheets. NPL can therefore choose its opening date rather than wait for a clean break; the effort depends on the number of senders with a balance on that day, which Finance has from the sheets, not on the number of historical transactions.

6 · Exit criteria and the go / no-go

  1. Ten consecutive business days with every check of Section 5 inside tolerance at the evening reconciliation, and no discrepancy open for more than one day during the run.
  2. Every project NPL runs today has had at least one deal through the app during the run, or a scripted test deal on staging where the weeks brought none (the dormant Evo products need none).
  3. The ten flows of the Scenario Catalogue have each been exercised at least once on staging, by a real deal or a scripted one: F1 Cash by token (collection or settlement); F2 USDT collection with wallet screening; F3 Receiver wallet screened before settlement; F4 SWIFT collection; F5 SWIFT settlement; F6 Returned transfer; F7 One-time local deposit account; F8 Local payouts for small amounts; F9 Bank cutoff; F10 Internal transfer inside the partner's platform.
  4. Every role has done its own work in the app: Operations has quoted, verified, instructed and prepared; Finance has approved, confirmed rate versions, reconciled and recorded a recovery or an offset; Management has approved an escalation, a configuration change and a user change.
  5. The visibility checks of the Roles & Visibility Matrix pass on staging with NPL's real configuration.
  6. The opening entries are complete and signed off by Finance: every project's balances in the app equal the sheets' as of the opening date, and every in-flight deal is in the app in its current state.
  7. A restore has been rehearsed: the staging database restored from a snapshot into a scratch instance, and the ledger checks run there.
  8. Management signs the go/no-go, on Finance's discrepancy log and the engineering checklist, and names the switch day. A no-go extends the run by whole weeks; nothing is rushed into the switch.
  9. Security and access: an Operations user tries to see NPL's margin, a pool or a ledger balance, and to approve a settlement, through the screens, the API, an export, a notification and the client journal — expected: denied everywhere; nothing changes; no hidden field reaches the browser. Runs it: Engineering. Signs off: Management.
  10. Concurrent actions: two Operations users edit the same deal; two Finance users approve the same settlement at once; a job is started twice — expected: exactly one succeeds; no duplicate posting; the second is refused with the current state (Lifecycle & Approvals 6a). Runs it: Engineering. Signs off: Engineering lead.
  11. Application outage mid-transaction: the application is stopped while a collection is being recorded and while a conversion is being instructed — expected: on restart nothing committed is lost, nothing half-done is posted, the deal shows its true state (Ledger Pattern P). Runs it: Engineering. Signs off: Operations.
  12. Duplicate submission: the same settlement instruction is sent twice because the first response was slow; the same collection is recorded twice — expected: the second is refused by its idempotency key; one posting. Runs it: Engineering. Signs off: Finance.
  13. Restore and recover: the database is restored to a point in time and the deals processed after that point are re-entered and reconciled under the recovery runbook (8a) — expected: no missing and no duplicate movement; the identities of the Ledger Posting Design hold; technical restore and resumption are two recorded sign-offs. Runs it: Engineering with Finance. Signs off: Management.

A basic vulnerability assessment of the staging environment is run before go-live, separately from UAT; the penetration test remains the optional add-on of the proposal, for NPL to decide (Architecture note, security operations).

8 · Rollback

Until the switch day, rollback is nothing: staging is discarded or the run extended. On the switch day, if the copy or the verification fails, production is left empty, staging goes back to read-write and the run continues; a new switch day is chosen. After the first live day, if the app cannot be used for a day, the team works from the frozen sheets plus the app's entries so far (the Architecture note's fallback) and enters the day into the app afterwards with the real timestamps in the evidence; the ledger is never edited to catch up, it is posted. A restore from backup is the last resort and a Management decision: the ledger is then the ledger as of the restore minute, and the gap is re-entered from the sheets.

8a · Recovery runbook, and the recovery objectives

NPL's decisions of 20 October: recovery inside one hour, not four; and three additions that are part of the design, not options. A replica and a workbook reduce how often the runbook is needed; they do not replace it, because re-entry and reconciliation still happen after any fallback.

The runbook

StepWhoWhat
1. DeclareFinance, EngineeringThe outage is declared with its time; Operations works from the fallback workbook (Operating Procedures A.12), recording every instruction given to a partner with the deal reference and the time.
2. Restore or fail overEngineeringA zone failure fails over to the standby automatically. Otherwise Engineering restores to the latest point in time; a restore of production is a Management decision recorded in the audit log. Technical restore is sign-off one.

5. ReconcileFinanceEvery partner's statement or balance is reconciled against the ledger (Section 5 checks; the reconciliation record) before the first new deal.
6. ResumeManagementResumption of processing is sign-off two, recorded separately from the technical restore, once step 5 agrees and every instruction given during the outage is accounted for.

10 · Assumptions and decisions for NPL

  1. Three to four weeks is the planned length of the run; the exit criteria, not the calendar, end it.
  2. The opening date is the first day of the parallel run, chosen by NPL; the sheets are reconciled as of the previous close and every open balance on that date is entered as a verified opening entry (Section 4). There is no backfill start date.
  3. Opening entries are posted from a Finance-verified, Management-approved list, with evidence; in-flight deals and the deals of the run are entered through the screens. No historical deals are re-entered, so the volume of history does not affect the run.
  4. The one reset of production is the one on NPL's architecture page; the copy from staging means production starts from the reconciled run's data, then live data after.
  5. Client journals write to copies during the run and to the real sheets from the switch day.
  6. The switch day is NPL's choice, a couple of days ahead, on the least traffic; the runbook of Section 7 does not depend on the weekday.
  7. Staging cost: a second small service and database for the weeks of the run, about the running cost again (Architecture & Stack Note).
