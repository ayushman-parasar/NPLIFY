NPLify · P0 Technical Baseline

Operating Procedures — one SOP per project

New XP Technologies Limited

28 October 2026 · Confidential

This document says how each of NPL's projects is run day to day in NPLify: who does what, in which order, with which approval and which evidence. It is written from NPL's own description of each project, its fee table and its payment-method steps, and from the model the application is built on, so every step names the record the application keeps. Part A is the procedures every project shares — the common procedures C1 to C11 for collecting, approving and paying out, with the outcome table C6a, and the standing procedures of the day. Part B is one standard operating procedure per project, which refers to Part A by number rather than repeating it.

Three roles, one rule. Operations runs deals: quotes, screens, collects, instructs conversions, prepares payouts, records what happened. Finance approves what Operations initiates, confirms the partner rates of the day, resolves exceptions, reconciles every evening and at month end. Management approves the heavier actions: anything above a limit, configuration, users, offsets, balance conversions, loss splits, covered fees. Every controlled action is maker-checker: the person who initiates never approves. Operations never sees NPL's margin, the partner's cost, the pool or a ledger balance; the sender sees only the sender-facing figures.

Part A · Common procedures

The common procedures below are the ten shared flows of the scenario catalogue (F1 Cash by token (collection or settlement); F2 USDT collection with wallet screening; F3 Receiver wallet screened before settlement; F4 SWIFT collection; F5 SWIFT settlement; F6 Returned transfer; F7 One-time local deposit account; F8 Local payouts for small amounts; F9 Bank cutoff; F10 Internal transfer inside the partner's platform), written as steps, and the Email OTP procedure C11. A project SOP cites them by code.

C1 · Cash by token — collection or payout

When: Any cash leg: INR, THB, JPY, AED or USD cash, in a named city. The same steps serve a collection and a payout with the roles switched.

WhoDoes
1. OperationsTakes the city from the sender (or receiver) first: the partner's rate and its agent depend on it. Shares the partner's rate for that city with the party; on a bank-side pair this happens before the partner's 14:30 cutoff.
2. OperationsOn the party's confirmation, asks the partner for the token (a local-currency bill whose serial number identifies the agent) and the agent's contact, and records both on the collection or the payout line.
3. OperationsPasses the token serial and the contact to the sender (collection) or relays the receiver's token to the partner and the agent (payout). If the partner replaces the agent or the token before the meeting, passes the new details on; the rate locked at quote does not change.
4. Sender / receiver and agentMeet; the agent shows the token; cash is handed over; the party keeps the token (collection) or the agent photographs it (payout).
5. OperationsAttaches the photo of the token as evidence; shares it with the partner to close the loop.
6. PartnerCounts and confirms the amount. A count that is short is recorded as a shortfall (collection: the deal continues on the counted amount; payout: topped up at the next payout or paid separately).
7. OperationsMarks the part counted and verifies it (collection) or marks the line confirmed (payout). The collected cash amount is rounded as the pair says (INR, THB, JPY to the thousand; AED cash to 100; USD cash in Dubai to 10).

Recorded: Token serial, agent contact, the token photo, counted amount and time; the city on the deal.

C2 · USDT collection with wallet screening

When: Every USDT collection on every project. Sender wallets are screened before any address is given out, except where the project allows a sender to skip it (some Evo senders).

WhoDoes
1. OperationsAsks the sender to declare the sending wallet (network and address).
2. OperationsScreens the wallet with the screening tool and records result, decision and evidence. A report that is clear under the provider's strict rulebook (nothing at threshold, no terrorism-financing trace, score in the clear band) is Operations' decision: accept, the partner's standard receiving wallet. Anything else goes to Management's second look, which decides accept, accept on the partner's alternate (elevated-band) wallet, or reject, with the reason, the report and the decision kept together; the approver is never the person who screened. A reject stands: it is lifted only by a new screening of the same address decided by Management, and nobody at NPL, Finance included, overrides it. A hold, freeze or decline by the partner or its bank is the partner's decision: NPL records it as the event it is, routes around it and records the partner's clearance reference when the partner lifts it (Roles & Visibility rule 11; Configuration Schema 4).
3. OperationsGives the sender the receiving address. If the partner lacks the sender's network (Ali has no ERC-20), gives NPL's own bridge wallet on that network; the funds are forwarded to the partner on a supported network as a hop, within the wallet's custody limit.
4. SenderSends the transfer and shares the transaction hash.
5. OperationsChecks the hash on Tronscan (TRC-20) or Etherscan (ERC-20): address, amount, confirmations. Attaches the result as evidence and shares the hash with the partner.
6. PartnerConfirms the funds are received.
7. OperationsVerifies the collection part. An overpayment becomes a sender credit; an underpayment leaves the deal in Collecting until Finance decides to wait or void.

Recorded: Screening record; sending address; hash and explorer result; the partner's confirmation.

C3 · Receiver wallet screened before a USDT payout

When: Every payout to a wallet: BF withdrawals, Peru, NPL-GR vendor payments, JTN and Jonas payouts in USDT, AK Personal withdrawals in USDT.

WhoDoes
1. OperationsTakes the receiver's wallet (network, address); screens it and records the report, the decision and the evidence on the account's destination approval, under the same rule as C2: a clear report is Operations' decision, anything else is Management's second look, a reject is never overridden.
2. OperationsPrepares the payout line to that account; Finance approves the payout (Section A.5).
3. PartnerExecutes the transfer and provides the hash.
4. OperationsShares the hash with the receiver; attaches it to the line.
5. ReceiverConfirms receipt; Operations marks the line confirmed.

Recorded: Screening record on the destination; the hash; the receiver's confirmation.

C4 · Bank transfer in (SWIFT; SEPA when offered)

When: Collections by bank transfer: Evo's other currencies, BF withdrawals (at Jeton), Peru, YN Remit and 66 Group where the sender pays by bank.

WhoDoes
1. OperationsCollects the sender's bank account and company profile (name, address, nature of business) and any KYC the partner asks for; asks the partner for a receiving account.
2. PartnerApproves the sender and provides the receiving account (its own vehicle's account, with the rail).
3. OperationsActivates the endpoint in the app once the rail is known; shares the details with the sender.
4. SenderExecutes the transfer and shares the proof.
5. OperationsAttaches the proof and shares it with the partner.
6. PartnerConfirms the incoming transfer.
7. OperationsVerifies the collection; the conversion follows the flow agreed with the sender before the transfer (Section A.4).

Recorded: Sender profile and KYC references; the receiving account; proof of transfer; the partner's confirmation.

C5 · Bank transfer out (SWIFT; SEPA when offered)

When: Payouts by bank transfer on every project that pays a bank account.

WhoDoes
1. OperationsBefore any collection: records the receiver's bank details and company profile (KYC if needed) and asks the partner to approve the destination and name the route. A quote-first deal cannot leave Inquiry until the destination is approved with the chosen partner.
2. OperationsAfter the collection is verified and converted: prepares the payout (amount, destination, narrative — the partner's vehicle, the customer's own entity or NPL's entity as the project says).
3. FinanceApproves the payout (Management also, above the settlement limit or for a third same-day slot). On a partner with a bank cutoff, a payout approved after the cutoff goes out on the next business day.
4. PartnerReleases the transfer and shares the slip.
5. OperationsShares the slip with the receiver; attaches it to the line.
6. ReceiverConfirms receipt; Operations marks the line confirmed. A request to cover the bank fee is a special case: Finance reviews and recommends, Management approves.
7. OperationsIf the transfer is not received: asks the partner to inquire through the banking channel (PT Sukses, PT Global); records the enquiry on the line until it is resolved.

Recorded: Destination approval; the narrative; the slip; the receiver's confirmation; any bank-fee decision.

C6 · Returned transfer

When: A bank rejects the payout and the money comes back to the partner, usually less a fee.

WhoDoes
1. OperationsRecords the return on the line: amount returned, bank fee kept, reason. The line's outcome becomes returned and the money is back in the client's balance at that partner.
2. FinanceDecides with the client: resend to the same destination, use the returned funds for another payout in the same group, or reconvert and return to the sender. A return to the sender is a return leg to the sender's own return account; any other destination needs Management approval with a reason.
3. OperationsPrepares the chosen payout; the bank fee is treated as the project says (waived, carried forward or absorbed) or by a one-off decision.

Recorded: Return record with amount, fee and reason; the follow-up payout.

C6a · Rejected payout — the four outcomes

When: The outcomes a returned transfer (C6) can take, each with who decides, what posts (Ledger Pattern F) and the evidence. The client's money is back in its balance at the partner the moment the return is recorded; nothing is cancelled.

WhoDoes
1. FinanceOutcome 1 — resend to the same destination, once the cause is fixed (a wrong reference, a closed intermediary): Finance approves the new line as a payout; the bank fee kept by the bank posts as the project's treatment says.
2. FinanceOutcome 2 — use the returned funds for another payout in the same receiver group: Finance approves the new line; the group's entitlement is unchanged.
3. Finance · ManagementOutcome 3 — return the money to the sender: a return leg to the sender's own return account, Finance approves; any other destination for the sender's money needs Management's approval with a reason.
4. ManagementOutcome 4 — pay a different receiver or hold the money: a payout outside the group is the cross-group right (Management); a hold keeps the money in the client's balance at the partner, on the exception list with its age, until Finance decides.

Recorded: The return record (amount, fee, reason); the approval of the chosen outcome; the follow-up line with its reference; the bank-fee decision where one was taken.

C7 · One-time local deposit account

When: Local-currency collections where the partner arranges an account for one transaction and one amount (INR for 66 Group, local deposits generally).

WhoDoes
1. OperationsAsks the partner for the account for this deal; records it as a single-use endpoint reserved for the deal with the expected amount.
2. OperationsShares name, account number, bank and the exact amount with the sender.
3. SenderDeposits at a branch or transfers locally; shares the proof.
4. OperationsAttaches the proof; shares it with the partner.
5. PartnerConfirms the funds are recognised; Operations verifies the collection.
6. Operations / Finance / ManagementIf the account is frozen: the partner asks the sender to confirm its own account is functional. Funds lost because of the sender's side are a loss event — Operations records it, Finance proposes the split, Management approves — and the collection is reversed only if Management unwinds the deal.

Recorded: Single-use endpoint with its deal and amount; proof of deposit; the partner's confirmation; any loss event.

C8 · Local payout for a small amount

When: Where the partner allows it (Singapore, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Thailand): the receiver gives a local account; the partner pays by cash deposit or local transfer.

WhoDoes
1. OperationsConfirms with the partner that the country and amount qualify; records the receiver's local account (single-use if given for this payout only).
2. OperationsPrepares the payout; Finance approves.
3. PartnerPays by cash deposit into the account or by local transfer and shares the proof. A deposit by the agent into the receiver's bank account is a local bank payment, not a cash hand-over: no token.
4. ReceiverConfirms receipt; Operations marks the line confirmed. Cash short is adjusted at the next payout or paid separately.

Recorded: Local account; proof of payment; the receiver's confirmation.

C9 · Bank cutoff (Ali and the other Asian partners)

When: Pairs that depend on the banking channel: conversions into EUR and other bank currencies at Ali, USD and SGD balances at Ali. Jeton and Aquanow have no cutoff.

WhoDoes
1. FinanceIn the morning: takes the partner's rate for the day, compares it with the market and confirms the rate version (a breach freezes the pair until Finance decides).
2. OperationsQuotes and locks the rate with the partner before 14:30 Hong Kong time; a deal locked before the cutoff converts the same day.
3. OperationsAfter the cutoff: the collection goes ahead on the morning's figures; the app stamps the conversion due date as the next business day (weekends and the partner's holidays skipped); the converted value shows as pending in the balance.
4. FinanceNext business day: if the partner's rate still holds the minimum margin, the conversion runs at that day's rate and NPL absorbs the difference; if not, asks the partner for a better rate or defers another day, with the reason recorded.
5. OperationsPayouts by bank on such a partner approved after the cutoff go out on the next business day. For an urgent request the partner may extend the cutoff by 30–60 minutes; Finance records the extension with the partner's agreement. On a Friday or before a holiday the partner may close an hour early.

Recorded: Rate version of the day; lock time, cutoff time and due date on the deal; any cutoff override.

C10 · Internal transfer inside the partner's platform

When: Aquanow moves EUR to BF's account on the same platform (and to Raeen's own Aquanow account for sub-account deals).

WhoDoes
1. OperationsPrepares the payout to the account whose rail is internal; Finance approves.
2. PartnerMoves the funds; BF recognises them at once; no bank fee.
3. OperationsMarks the line confirmed on the receiver's confirmation (often the same day).

Recorded: The internal transfer reference; the receiver's confirmation.

C11 · Email OTP — the customer's approval of the order

When: Any quote-first deal on a project with Email OTP switched on. The customer approves the exact order by a one-time code sent by email and pasted in the sender's registered chat group; the code is the acceptance (Lifecycle & Approvals 2e). A required sender whose approver address and chat group are not entered and approved cannot be quoted while the switch is on; the screen says why. A sender marked not required (its customer will not name an approver address) is quoted and approved in its chat group as before the feature.

WhoDoes
1. OperationsPrepares the quote as usual (Finance approves it where the thresholds require) and presses Send for customer approval. For a sender marked not required nothing else happens: the quote goes out as today, the approval is taken in the chat group and the issue records it (step 7). For a required sender one email leaves for its registered approver address: the quote as rendered, a one-time code with its expiry and the code box's sentence that it is single use, for this quote issue only and void if the order changes, the first and last four characters of the collection destination, and, at or above the step-up amount, the instruction to paste the code and the order amount. No link, never the full account or wallet.
2. CustomerPastes the code in the sender's registered chat group; at or above the step-up amount, the order amount as well. A reply to the email, or a message in the group, with 'reject' and the reason is a rejection.
3. OperationsEnters the code (and the amount where required). The server validates it: right code, right issue, unexpired, unused; five wrong attempts lock it and a locked, expired or voided code needs a new send. Attaches a screenshot of the customer's message as evidence. The group is customer approved.
4. OperationsSends the full destination in the registered chat group; the customer's standing rule is to pay only if it matches the partial identifier in the email. The collection then follows C2, C4 or C7 as usual.
5. OperationsIf nothing arrives from the registered group within the tripwire window (default 4 hours), verifies with the customer through a known contact outside the chat. A code whose chat evidence cannot be produced from the registered group blocks collection and opens an exception.
6. Operations · FinanceA change of a sender's approver address or chat group is a settings change: Operations enters, Finance approves (never the same person); the new address is active only after its verification code comes back through that sender's group; that sender's open codes are voided.
7. Operations · Finance · ManagementMarking a sender not required (the customer declines to name an approver address) is the same settings change with Management's approval added, the customer's reason recorded; the sender is then quoted as today, every quote issue records 'approval taken in chat, Email OTP not required for this sender', and the sender appears as exempt on the senders list and on the quarterly review (A.10).

Recorded: The email snapshot, the code reference, the chat screenshot, the approval stamp (user and time); a rejection with its reason verbatim; for a sender marked not required, the line 'approval taken in chat, Email OTP not required for this sender' on the quote issue.

Part A · Standing procedures of the day

A.1 · Rates of the day

  • Operations enters each partner's rate for the pairs in use (Ali in the morning for bank-side pairs; Jeton and Aquanow when quoting) as quoted, with its direction, and Finance confirms it; the app normalises it and compares it with the market feed (Kraken, then CoinMarketCap).
  • A version whose spread breaches the project's threshold freezes quoting and conversion on that partner × pair until Finance approves it with a reason, rejects it, or asks the partner for a better rate (a new version). A version outside the absolute tolerance in either direction, better or worse than the market, is flagged: it prices nothing and instructs nothing until Finance confirms it with a reason or rejects it, while the last usable version keeps serving; a quote or conversion attempted against a flagged version is refused and the refusal logged (Calculation Specification 2a). Operations sees only usable, flagged or frozen.
  • With both market feeds down, Operations enters a manual rate with evidence and Finance confirms it; nothing prices until a rate exists.

A.2 · Quoting

  • Operations picks the sender, the receiver group, the partner × pair and the amount mode (fixed amount in, or target amount out). The app applies the fee structure in force (and any sender override or tier), prices on the market rate or the partner's rate as the structure says, and produces the sender-facing figures.
  • A quote below the minimum margin needs Finance approval before it is sent. The quote package is rendered from the project's template and sent; the package is kept with the figures it printed.
  • Validity runs per the project (freshness, validity, grace). Before validity ends Operations may request an extension, approved by Finance, any number of times. After validity: the grace rule applies; beyond it the sender gets a new deal. Honouring an expired rate on a new deal is a Finance approval.
  • Collect-first senders have no quote: the deal is created when the money arrives and priced at conversion.

A.3 · Collecting

  • By the common procedure the method calls for: C2 (USDT), C4 (bank), C1 (cash), C7 (one-time local account). Every part is verified by Operations with its evidence; the deal moves to Collected when the full amount (or the agreed partial) is verified.
  • The collection must land on an endpoint of the partner entity stamped on the deal, of the kind the leg's method requires. The app refuses anything else.
  • On a project with Email OTP switched on, no collection is recorded until the customer's code has been validated for the current quote issue (C11); a required sender whose approver address and chat group are not entered and approved cannot be quoted at all, and the screen says why; a sender marked not required collects as before the feature, its quote issue recording that the approval was taken in chat.

A.4 · Converting

  • Operations instructs the conversion once the deal is Collected (on a bank-side pair, not before the conversion due date). Finance approves. The receiver's fee part is taken at conversion; the sender's part was taken in the price.
  • The partner's actual conversion is recorded; a difference from the expected figure beyond the project's tolerance opens a variance exception that Finance resolves (Management above the project's limit). The deal does not wait for it.
  • Finance may keep NPL's margin unconverted in the collection currency (and later offset it between partners); otherwise the margin converts with the client's money and rests at the partner.

A.5 · Paying out (settlement)

  • Operations prepares the settlement: lines to approved destinations, within the group's entitlement, rounded as the pair says (bank transfers carry cents; cash rounds to the unit). The narrative is the one configured for the route.
  • Finance approves; Management also above the settlement limit, for a third same-day slot, or when a counterparty receiver is paid from another group's money (an offset). With only one Finance user on shift, Management countersigns.
  • Release by the partner discharges the promise; the receiver's confirmation closes the line; a short delivery opens a shortfall; a return re-raises the promise (C6).

A.6 · End of day

  • Finance reconciles: balances per partner × currency against the partner's view, open deals and their states, the day's margin, the exception list (variance exceptions, frozen pairs, open offsets, custody aging, pending conversions, unconfirmed payouts, open loss recoveries, unconfirmed journal rows).
  • Operations clears what it owns on the exception list; anything that needs a decision is escalated to Finance before the next day's first deal.

A.7 · Month end

  • Finance reconciles the rebate accruals against each partner's statement (Jeton 0.4 % of the amount, 0.3 % on Novi; Aquanow 0.3 %) — only NPLify's own deals, never the partner's whole statement — and the markup-share accruals against each party's working (Raeen); records what was received; settles what NPL owes.
  • Finance reviews earnings resting at partners and proposes offsets between partners (Management approves) or leaves them for the partner's fee cycle.
  • Finance checks the month's tier volumes (GDC) and the exposure ceilings (AK Personal, any held balance).

A.8 · Exceptions that have their own procedure

  • Reroute (partner unavailable after quote): Operations requests, Finance approves before conversion; Management approves a reroute after conversion and whenever the contingency route set in configuration is used; client money in NPL's own wallet is reported daily until it moves on.
  • Void: before money moved, Finance approves; after collection (refund, credit or hold), Management approves.
  • Loss event (frozen account, cash short and unrecoverable, wrong-chain transfer): Operations records it the same day; the whole loss is booked to NPL and the client made whole; Finance proposes the split with the partner, the sender and the client; Management approves; each share is recovered and recorded separately.
  • Shortfall: topped up at the next payout, or by its own payout; a top-up from NPL's pool is a Management approval.
  • Balance conversion (a held balance into another currency): Finance requests, Management approves; on a bank-side pair the cutoff applies.
  • A leg on hold (a partner's hold, freeze or decline, or NPL's own screening awaiting Management's second look): the leg keeps its state and the server refuses every movement of the money — conversion, balance conversion, settlement line, refund, reroute forward, return — for every role; recording, screening, evidence, partner messages and a void before money moved stay allowed. A partner hold ends only when Finance records the partner's clearance with its reference; a screening hold only with Management's second look or a new clear screening. The one movement under hold is the one the partner instructs in writing, recorded with its reference and approved by Management, never while the partner's hold is on the balance itself. A partner hold is chased daily from the exception list (Lifecycle & Approvals 2c).
  • An instruction with outcome unknown (the partner has not acknowledged a conversion, settlement line or reroute forward by the time-out): Operations raises the partner reference inquiry the same day and records the answer with its evidence; Finance obtains the partner's statement; nothing is sent again by anyone meanwhile. Executed: the instruction is marked against the partner's reference and the line continues. Not received: Management closes it as rejected on the partner's written confirmation and Finance authorises the one retry under a new partner reference, recorded as a retry of the held line. Escalated to Management if the partner has not answered by its next statement or within two business days (Lifecycle & Approvals 2d and 8a).
  • Bank account details (a receiving account, a partner's bank endpoint, a settlement rail, a sender's bank source): Operations enters them in full from the bank's letter or confirmation, with the document as evidence; Finance or Management approves, never the person who entered them; the row becomes active and the account, endpoint or rail that points at it may become active. The details are read in full by all three roles and every full read is logged (Roles & Visibility rule 10).
  • A dormant route (a partner pair in status dormant): it prices nothing and accepts no collection or settlement line. Before it is used again Finance runs the reactivation checklist — the partner's current confirmation of the product, the destination approvals, the rates of the day, the fee structure validated against the current agreement, the test cases of the Test Strategy run on staging — and Management sets the pair active. A route unused for the project's route_unused_days threshold is put on the quarterly review to be set dormant or kept (Scenario Catalogue; Configuration Schema, partner pair).
  • The opening date (opening entries, in-flight deals, suspense lines): the procedure is Section 4 of the Cutover Approach and is not repeated here.

A.10 · People joining and leaving

  • A new account is created on the day the person starts, with one role and the person named behind it (USER.person_ref); Management approves. Nobody holds two accounts that can approve each other's requests.
  • A leaver's account is closed the same day, before the end of the day's work: the role removed, the account disabled, the person removed from the share lists of the two fallback workbooks, and any pending request of theirs dropped or reassigned. Management does it and a second Management user confirms (Roles rule 7); the audit log carries both.
  • Every quarter Management confirms the list of users and their roles, the rights per controlled action, the share lists of the two workbooks against the user list, and, per project, the senders' registered approver addresses and chat groups for Email OTP and the senders marked not required (Roles & Visibility rule 12, rule 13 and 5a).

A.11 · The health checks

  • Every morning Finance reads the alarm mailbox and the exception list before the first deal. A job that stopped after three attempts or a run the sweep found missing is an exception until Engineering has cleared it and the job-health line on the monitor is green again; the alarm names the job, the time and the event, never a figure (Architecture & Stack 4 and 6).
  • Every week the backup-verification job restores last week's backup into a scratch database by itself and runs the restore checks (the ledger identities, every evidence reference, every type-plus-id reference); the result is recorded with its time, and every month Engineering reviews those results and signs them off. Every year the rehearsal is the region case: the standby database promoted in the second region, the application started there, every step timed, and the measured recovery time and data loss reported to NPL (Cutover Approach 8a; Test Strategy G-REST).
  • The two fallback workbooks are checked weekly for freshness (an export every five minutes) and for their share lists (A.10).

A.12 · Working from the fallback workbook

  • When the application cannot be used, Finance declares the outage with its time and tells the team; from then on Operations works from the Operations workbook (open deals and their states, cutoffs, pending approvals, the day's partner rates as entered, the amount available to pay out per partner and currency) and Finance from the Finance workbook (balances, pool, margins, reconciliation figures). Both are refreshed every five minutes while the application runs, so they start from the last five minutes before the outage.
  • Every instruction given to a partner during the outage is written in the workbook with the deal reference, the partner's reference and the time, and the partner's confirmation is kept; nothing is instructed twice for the same promise, and nothing new is quoted unless Management says so.
  • When the application is back, the recovery runbook of Cutover Approach 8a applies: Engineering identifies the last committed transaction, Operations and Finance re-enter everything after it from the workbook and the partner confirmations in time order, with the real times in the evidence, and Finance reconciles every partner balance before the first new deal. Management records the resumption.

Part C · Starting a new project

  1. Classify it against Part B: which SOP is it closest to — an invoice-payment project (YN Remit), a funding project with a fixed fee (BF), a shared-markup arrangement (BF Sub), a cash project (JTN, Jonas), a held-balance account (AK Personal), or NPL's own desk (NPL-GR)?
  2. Configure it from the Configuration Schema: client and project, parties and their accounts, partner configuration with the pairs and the fee structure (fixed, variable with a cap, tiered, shared), destination approvals, templates. Finance proposes, Management approves; the validation must pass before the first deal.
  3. If it is cloned from an archetype or another project, the clone copies structure, not agreements. The new project records its source, and every copied row carries it until a user confirms or changes the row. The reconfirm list is left empty on purpose and must be set by hand: on the project, the client, the name and label, the exposure ceiling, the settlement policy and every threshold with an amount or currency; per partner pair, the fee percentage and its parts, the tiers, the minimum and maximum markup and the rate basis; per partner block, the partner entities, endpoints, settlement sending entities and rails, destination approvals, rebate rule and markup shares; the parties, accounts, bank account details and allow-lists, including each sender's Email OTP approver address, chat group and requirement, which are copied as rows to confirm like the rest and never activate an exemption on the new project without Management's approval there. A partner's cutoff, time zone and holidays are never copied. Activation is refused with a field-level list of what is missing until every copied row is confirmed, every referenced version is the one in force on the activation date, and every block's route is complete (Configuration Schema 1a and rule 15).
  4. Write its SOP on the template of Part B — at a glance, configuration, procedure, rules, checks — citing the common procedures by code. Any step not covered by Part A is a question for Management before the project runs, not a workaround.
  5. Run the first deal with Finance on the screen, reconcile it the same evening, and only then hand the project to the normal rota.

Figures in the examples are NPL's own from its fee table where quoted; the rest are illustrative.
