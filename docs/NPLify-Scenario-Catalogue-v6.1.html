NPLify · P0 Technical Baseline

Scenario Catalogue

New XP Technologies Limited

28 October 2026 · Confidential

Every scenario NPL runs today, grouped by client and project: 27 scenarios across 6 client groups. Each scenario is given as NPL described it (the story), the fee and the operational flow it uses, and the parts of the model that carry it. The ten operational flows the scenarios share are defined once in Part 2 and referenced by number (F1–F10); Part 3 lists the guided walkthroughs that trace a deal through the model entity by entity, as they appear on the interactive map; Part 4 walks one deal through the model class by class (X1–X7).

How to read this pack

Four map pages are now part of the pack as documents of their own, and are the place to start: the Top-Down Map (from the business, through the data model and the deal states, to the scenarios and the reading order), the Deal Flow on one page (Quote, Collect, Convert, Settle, with the roles and the money path), the Workflow Tree (8 classes, 27 sub-classes, 96 procedures, each naming who initiates, who approves, the codes it is written under and the records it touches) and the Workflow Tree on one page. Read them in that order, then this document. Every worked transaction (T), acceptance vector (L, B), walkthrough (W, X), flow (F) and procedure (C, A, D) has one identifier, listed in the Reading Companion's identifier register; the Test Strategy & Coverage document says which test proves each procedure.

Dormant scenarios: disabled by default, and the reactivation checklist

A dormant scenario (today 2.8 and 2.9) is listed so that the configuration, the fee sheet and this catalogue agree, not so that it can be chosen. Its rows are kept and disabled: the partner pair carries status dormant, its collection endpoints and settlement rails are inactive, its destination approvals are suspended, and its fee structure and tiers keep their last version. A dormant route is not offered on the quote screen, and the server refuses a quote, a collection instruction and a settlement line on it, for every role and channel, with the reason "route dormant". A dormant route needs no deal in the parallel run (Cutover Approach, Section 6) and is not a test of the Test Strategy until it is reactivated.

Reactivation is a configuration change like any other (Finance proposes, Management approves) with a checklist whose evidence is attached to the proposal; a step without evidence keeps the route dormant. The route becomes active only when every step is done, the partner configuration block passes the validation rules of the Configuration Schema (Section 6, rules 13 and 15) and Management activates it.

Email OTP: how a customer approves an order (optional, per project)

Today a customer's consent to an order is a message in the chat group, outside the system. Email OTP is an optional feature NPL switches on per project (none of the 27 scenarios above has it on today; any project can). With it on, the story of every quote-first deal gains one step between the quote and the collection: the customer approves the exact order by a one-time code. The design is the reviewer's change request CR-01 of October 2026; the rules are in Lifecycle & Approvals 2e, the settings in Configuration Schema §3 and rule 16, the protection of the settings in Roles & Visibility rule 13.

The story, with Client 1 of the Configuration Schema's worked example. Operations prepares a quote for USD 60,000 of USDT to EUR at Ali and, since it is above the project's margin threshold, Finance approves it. Operations presses Send for customer approval. One email leaves for Sender A's registered approver address (each sender of the project has its own approver and its own chat group): the quote as rendered (deal reference and issue number, pair, amount in, fees, amount out, collection method, validity), a one-time code with its expiry in Hong Kong time, and the first and last four characters of Ali's receiving wallet. No link, no full address. The customer pastes the code and, because USD 60,000 is above the project's step-up amount of USD 50,000, the order amount too, into Sender A's registered Telegram group 'Client 1 · Sender A × NPL'. Operations enters both; the server checks the code against its hash, the issue, the expiry, the attempt count and the amount, and stamps the approval with the user and the time; Operations attaches a screenshot of the customer's message. The deal is customer approved. Operations now sends the full wallet address in the registered group; the customer compares its first and last four characters with the email before paying. From here the story is the ordinary one: collection, screening, conversion, settlement.

The acceptance test is S-OTP (Test Strategy): no email alone ever approves; no code validates twice, after expiry, after a change to the order or against another deal; the amount is required above the step-up threshold; a pending change to a sender's approver address blocks sending for that sender and voids its live codes; every approved deal shows its snapshot, code reference and chat evidence; a late approval goes through the drift test; the full destination always matches the emailed partial; a code with no evidence from the registered group raises the tripwire and blocks collection.

Part 2 · Shared operational flows

Defined once and referenced by number from the scenarios above. The step-by-step version of each, with who does what and the evidence recorded, is in the Operating Procedures (common procedures C1–C11).

F1Cash by token (collection or settlement)

Collection: the partner issues a token (a local-currency bill) and the agent's contact; the sender meets the agent, hands over the cash, keeps the token and shares its photo; the partner counts and confirms. The rate is the one locked at quote even if the hand-over or the count slips to the next day; the partner may replace the agent and the token before the meeting, and NPL passes the new details to the sender. Settlement: the receiver provides the token up front to NPL, NPL to the partner, the partner to the agent; the agent takes the token, hands over the cash, photographs the token for the partner, who passes it to NPL and NPL to the receiver. A cash-in deal paid out in USDT has no cutoff; one paid out by bank does

In the model COLLECTION.token_ref (replaceable before hand-over), agent_contact_ref, evidence_ref (the photo), counted_at, states token_issued, handed_over, counted, verified; DEAL.cash_location; PARTNER_RATE_VERSION.location; SETTLEMENT_LINE.token_ref (the receiver's token) and the agent's photo as evidence

F2USDT collection with wallet screening

Sender declares a wallet; NPL screens it; standard or alternate receiving wallet by risk; sender shares the hash; NPL verifies on chain; partner confirms. Where a sender cannot share a wallet up front (Evo and other projects) NPL arranges an alternative address: each partner has designated wallets for such cases, and where the partner does not support the network (Ali has no ERC-20) NPL collects on its own alternate wallet on that network and forwards to the partner on a network it supports (TRC-20), without the risk exposure

In the model WALLET_SCREENING (Operations); COLLECTION.sending_address, wallet_screening_id, screening_skipped; COLLECTION_RECEIVING_ENDPOINT.risk_band for the partner's designated wallets; OWN_WALLET as a bridge endpoint forwarded to the partner as a hop — the bridge role of OWN_WALLET covers this

F3Receiver wallet screened before settlement

NPL screens the receiver's wallet, the partner executes, the hash is shared, the receiver confirms

In the model WALLET_SCREENING subject receiver_wallet; DESTINATION_APPROVAL.wallet_screening_id

F4SWIFT collection

Partner approves the sender from company profile and KYC and provides an account; proof of transfer exchanged; partner confirms; conversion per the agreed flow

In the model COLLECTION_SENDING_ENTITY; bank endpoint active only with its rail and matching the leg; COLLECTION.evidence_ref, verified_by, verified_at

F5SWIFT settlement

Partner approves the destination and route before collection; transfer after collection; slip shared with the receiver; receiver confirms; on special cases the client asks for the bank fee to be covered — Finance reviews and recommends, Management approves; an unreceived transfer is chased through the banking channel

In the model DESTINATION_APPROVAL; SETTLEMENT, SETTLEMENT_RAIL, SETTLEMENT_SENDING_ENTITY; CONFIRMATION; BANK_FEE_EVENT with a FEE_DECISION recommended by Finance and approved by Management

F6Returned transfer

Resend, reuse the returned funds for another transfer, or reconvert and return to the sender

In the model SETTLEMENT_RETURN; reversing posting; return leg to the sender_return receiver

F7One-time local deposit account

The partner provides an ad hoc account for one transaction and amount; deposit or local transfer; proof exchanged; partner recognises the funds; a frozen account caused on the sender's side is a reversal of the collection; a loss can be partial or shared

In the model COLLECTION_RECEIVING_ENDPOINT usage single_use, reserved_for_deal_id, expected_amount; LOSS_EVENT with the four-way split, EXP_LOSS; COLLECTION state reversed

F8Local payouts for small amounts

Receiver gives a local account where the partner allows it (SG, HK, Indonesia, Thailand); the partner pays by cash deposit into that account or by local transfer and shares proof; receiver confirms. A cash deposit by the agent into the receiver's bank account is a local bank payment, not a cash hand-over: no token. Cash short at hand-over is adjusted later or paid separately

In the model RECEIVING_ENTITY_ACCOUNT usage single_use; SHORTFALL topped up next settlement or by its own SETTLEMENT; LOSS_EVENT if unrecoverable

F9Bank cutoff

Per partner where the partner has one — Ali and other Asian partners; Jeton and Aquanow have none, so their cutoff is empty and nothing is bound. For pairs with a bank side only: binds the rate lock and the outgoing settlement; locked before 14:30 GMT+8 is same day, after it next business day at that day's rate (unless locked within the previous day's cutoff); extendable 30-60 minutes if the partner agrees; closed early on Fridays or before a holiday

Part 3 · Guided walkthroughs

Each walkthrough lights up the path a deal takes on the interactive map, one entity at a time. They are listed here so the catalogue is complete on paper.

W1 · Quote-first deal, end to end (20 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
PROJECTOne arrangement with the client (CLIENT → PROJECT). Its policies (quote freshness and validity, grace, fee treatment, settlement policy and exposure cap) govern everything below.
SENDEROperator picks the sender. The receiver groups this sender may pay are listed in SENDER_RECEIVER_ALLOW.
RECEIVER_GROUPThe entitled group. One of its receivers’ accounts must hold an approved destination (DESTINATION_APPROVAL) with the chosen partner before the deal leaves Inquiry.
PARTNER_PAIRChoosing partner × currency pair is part of pricing. Partner and partner entity are fixed per leg.
FEE_STRUCTUREThe versioned client-side fee for that partner × pair is stamped on the deal group.
RATE_COMPARISONCreated lazily for partner × pair × date by the first deal of the day.
MARKET_RATEThe market snapshot from the pair’s prioritised rate source.
PARTNER_RATE_VERSIONThe partner’s rate version for the day; the breach check may freeze the pair until Finance decides.
DEAL_GROUPThe client-facing instruction: pair as the sender sees it, sender rate, fee, reference. Every deal belongs to one; this one has a single leg.
DEALLeg 1, forward, funded by a collection. Priced once; sent / valid / grace timestamps and quote_hash recorded. A sender who asks for more time before expiry gets Finance-approved extensions, as many as needed, at the same price; after expiry a new deal group, which may be priced on the old rate by an approved override.
QUOTE_PACKAGEThe rendered quote as sent: template version, channel, printed sender-facing figures, sent_at (starts validity) and the hash that becomes the leg’s quote_hash. A re-issue is a new row; a new price is a new deal group.
DESTINATION_APPROVALDestination approval precedes collection: an approved account of the entitled group with this partner.
COLLECTIONFunds arrive in 1..n parts into a COLLECTION_RECEIVING_ENDPOINT of the leg’s partner entity (bank or wallet by kind); cash with instructions on the deal.
CONVERSIONThe partner converts against the exact rate version. actual_out is booked rounded as the pair says; a dropped fraction stays as client dust, a round-up is NPL’s cost; the receiver fee is taken here; NPL’s margin is captured now and never again at risk.
LEDGER_TRANSACTIONOne balanced transaction; margin recognition is the only client → company ownership flip.
SETTLEMENTPays the balance, rounded as the pair says, from the partner’s holding through a SETTLEMENT_RAIL with a SETTLEMENT_SENDING_ENTITY narrative.
SETTLEMENT_LINEOne or many destination accounts. Paying a receiver in another group creates the offset in the same action.
CONFIRMATIONThe receiver confirms the amount received: full or short.
SHORTFALLIf short, a shortfall opens against the receiver and is topped up by a later settlement.
BANK_FEE_EVENTPost-confirmation true-up of expected vs actual bank fee: waived, carried forward or absorbed.

W2 · Collect-first deal (6 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
COLLECTIONFunds arrive before any quote. Attribution needs an existing SENDER_RECEIVER_ALLOW entry; the sender is picked from the configured list.
DEAL_GROUPCreated with entry_mode collect_first; the sender is promised nothing.
DEALThe single leg is born in state Collected with priced_at = conversion time; no quote clocks.
PARTNER_RATE_VERSIONThe rate version is taken at conversion rather than stamped at quote.
CONVERSIONConverted against that version; actual_out booked; margin captured.
SETTLEMENTThen the same whole-unit settlement as any other deal.

W3 · Rejected payout and return leg (10 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
SETTLEMENTUSDT was converted to GBP at PT and paid out to the receiver’s account.
SETTLEMENT_RETURNThe receiving or intermediary bank rejects the transfer. The GBP returns to PT minus bank fees; the line’s outcome becomes returned and a reversing transaction puts the money back into the client’s balance.
BANK_FEE_EVENTThe bounce fee is booked as expected vs actual, waived (client) or absorbed (NPL) per the project’s treatment.
GROUP_ENTITLEMENTWith the line reversed, the group’s entitlement is restored. Option B stops here: the GBP stays in balance for later payouts to receivers in the same group; another group would be an approved offset.
DEALOption A: a return leg on the same deal group. leg_type return, funding_source returned_settlement, GBP → USDT at PT, priced at conversion. NPL’s original margin is untouched; the FX loss falls on the client’s funds.
FEE_DECISIONMarkup on the return leg is applied or waived case by case, with an approver.
CONVERSIONThe reverse conversion, owned by the return leg, using PT’s rate version for GBP → USDT.
RECEIVERThe destination is the sender_return receiver linked to the parent leg’s sender. Any other receiver needs Management approval, a reason, and a prior destination approval.
RECEIVING_ENTITY_ACCOUNTThe sender’s USDT wallet is one of that receiver’s accounts; it is chosen at payout and need not be the wallet the sender paid from.
SETTLEMENT_LINEThe USDT goes out as an ordinary payout line to that account, clearing the entitlement its return leg credited to the parent leg’s group.

W4 · Two-leg route via a deal group (9 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
DEAL_GROUPThe sender wants INR → EUR. Ali can collect INR but cannot reach EUR, so the group will have two legs. The group holds the one price the sender saw.
DEALLeg 1: INR → USDT at Ali, forward, funded by the collection. Partner fixed per leg.
CONVERSIONAli converts to USDT; margin on leg 1 is captured here.
RECEIVERJeton’s collection wallet is registered as a RECEIVER of kind partner_transit, so the hop is an ordinary payout.
SETTLEMENTAli pays the USDT to the transit receiver’s account. Entitlement passes through the transit group.
PARTNER_CONFIGLeg 2 is a second DEAL on the same group at Jeton: USDT → EUR, funding_source prior_leg, no collection.
PARTNER_RATE_VERSIONJeton’s rate version for USDT → EUR prices leg 2 at conversion.
SETTLEMENT_LINEJeton pays the EUR to the real receiver’s account. Margin across the route = group quote − both partner conversions.
GROUP_ENTITLEMENTThe entitled group’s tally reconciles once leg 2 converts and pays out.

W5 · Ad-hoc balance conversion (8 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
PROJECT_BALANCEThe client holds 5K SGD and 10K USDT at PT and must pay 15K USDT.
BALANCE_CONVERSIONFinance converts SGD → USDT at PT. Not a deal: no sender, receiver or collection. It names the group it serves (chosen split).
PARTNER_RATE_VERSIONThe same rate procedure applies: partner rate version, market snapshot, breach check.
FEE_DECISIONMarkup is optional, decided case by case with an approver.
CONVERSIONOwned by the balance conversion; actual_out rounded as the pair says, the residual kept as dust.
ENTITLEMENT_REATTRIBUTIONThe served group’s SGD entitlement becomes USDT entitlement. Any excess over its SGD entitlement is another group’s flow, booked as an approved offset.
LEDGER_TRANSACTIONSource BalanceConversion: debit client due USDT, credit client due SGD; an earnings posting only if markup was applied.
SETTLEMENTThe 15K USDT goes out as an ordinary settlement.

W6 · Reroute, custody and recover dues (6 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
DEALA priced leg hits a partner problem: unavailable, limit reached, holiday, compliance hold.
REROUTEThe only way the partner pair or partner entity changes after quote. Before collection, after collection or after conversion: after collection the outcome is via the own wallet or a refund to the sender; after conversion the balance comes back through the own wallet to the alternate partner. Finance approves a reroute before conversion; Management approves one after conversion and ratifies every emergency action.
OWN_WALLETContingency custody. CL.OWN.*.HELD may be debited in four cases only (a reroute before conversion, the balance of a reroute after conversion, an own-desk leg, a bridge collection); custody_max_hours bounds it.
PARTNER_CONFIGThe alternate partner must have applicable_for_reroute set. NPL’s cut is captured at whichever partner converts, so nothing is withheld.
EARNINGS_RECEIVABLEWith forward_and_recover_dues, NPL forwards less than the full amount and reduces its receivable at that partner by the same figure: own wallet cash up, receivable down, no income. The partner’s agreement is recorded.
CUSTODYThe custody aging view (collections under open reroutes plus recovered dues resting in the wallet, minus forwarded) is the standing control.

W7 · Rate day and breach approval (7 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
RATE_SOURCEKraken, CMC, OANDA or XE, each with a staleness threshold.
PAIR_RATE_SOURCEThe prioritised source chain for each currency pair.
MARKET_RATECaptured rate per source and pair; cross-source slippage is a query over these.
RATE_COMPARISONOne per partner × pair × date, with 2w and 4w average spreads built from confirmed versions only. A partner may give a new rate later the same day.
PARTNER_RATE_VERSIONBreached when spread > avg_2w × (1 + variance_threshold_rel). Quoting and conversion on that partner × pair freeze; Finance may also ask for a better rate.
APPROVALFinance approves with a reason or rejects. Approver ≠ initiator.
USERRoles: ops, finance, management. Operations never sees derived-economics fields.

W8 · Ledger and balances (7 steps)

EntityWhat happens there
CONVERSIONA source event (collection, conversion, balance conversion, settlement, return, confirmation, bank fee event, reroute, markup share, rebate, loss event, adjustment).
LEDGER_TRANSACTIONOne transaction per event, append-only; a correction is a reversing transaction.
LEDGER_POSTINGDebit and credit lines that sum to zero per currency, tagged client or company and by cost component.
LEDGER_ACCOUNTAccounts coded OWNER.HOLDER.CCY.PURPOSE; client due balances are one account per project × partner × currency. ADVANCE no longer exists.
PROJECT_BALANCEConversions in, balance conversions across currencies, returns back in, settlements out. Never stored; carries dust below one unit.
GROUP_ENTITLEMENTPer group and currency: forward legs’ client_net ± re-attributions − paid + returned. Σ groups = Σ client balance.
OFFSETPositive = owed, negative = holding another group’s flow. Zero by construction in a single-group project.
