How to read this document

Every account in the ledger is a jar with a label. Some jars hold money (at a partner, in NPL's wallet). Some hold promises (what NPL has undertaken to deliver, what someone owes NPL). Two hold NPL's results: what it has earned, and what it has cost. Every event moves value out of one jar and into another, and the amounts always match, so nothing ever appears from nowhere or disappears.

Each example is one event, shown as a table. The columns:

  • Account — the jar, written as owner · place · currency · purpose. CL jars hold the client's money; CO jars are NPL's.
  • Debit / Credit — the bookkeeper's two columns. You can ignore them.
  • In / Out — the same thing in a word: IN means value goes to this jar, OUT means it comes from this jar. The word after the dot says what kind of jar it is.
  • Owner — whose value it is, the client's or NPL's. Component — what kind of value: principal (the money itself), earnings, a cost, a fee, and so on.
  • Why — the reason for the line, in a few words.

Read the explanation on the right first, then look for each sentence in the table. A tall table has its explanation above it instead.

Pattern A — Direct collection, conversion and accumulation

The everyday deal. A sender pays a partner, the partner converts the money, and the converted money waits at the partner until it is paid out. The fee is taken at the moment of conversion, and that is the only moment when some of the client's money becomes NPL's.

AccountDebitCreditIn / OutCcyOwnerComponentWhy
CL.ALI.USDT.COLLECTED10,000.00IN · moneyUSDTclientprincipalclient money now sits at Ali, unconverted; the account is per partner, the endpoint only says which vehicle's wallet
CL.PROJECT.USDT.PAYABLE10,000.00OUT · promiseUSDTclientprincipalNPL owes this onward on the client's behalf
What is happening
A sender has just paid 10,000 USDT into Ali's wallet. Two things are now true at once: the money is sitting at Ali (money IN to the "Ali, USDT" jar) and NPL has promised to deliver it onward for the client (a promise OUT of the "we owe the receivers" jar). Nothing is earned yet. The money is the client's; NPL is only holding it.

AccountDebitCreditIn / OutCcyOwnerComponentWhy
CL.PROJECT.USDT.PAYABLE10,000.00IN · promiseUSDTclientprincipalthe USDT obligation is discharged by the conversion
CL.ALI.USDT.COLLECTED10,000.00OUT · moneyUSDTclientprincipalthe USDT left the collected position
CL.ALI.EUR.DUE8,820.00IN · moneyEURclientprincipalclient net, waiting to be settled (EUR pairs round exact: cents are paid out)
CO.ALI.EUR.POOL90.00IN · moneyEURcompanyearningsNPL's margin, physically still at Ali (EARNINGS_RECEIVABLE)
CL.PROJECT.EUR.PAYABLE8,820.00OUT · promiseEURclientprincipalowed to the receiver group, now in EUR
CO.NONE.EUR.EARN_GROSS90.00OUT · earnedEURcompanyearningsmargin recognised: the client's claim shrinks from 10,000 USDT to its EUR entitlement, the difference is NPL's
What is happening
Ali turns the USDT into euros. The USDT promise is torn up (IN · promise) and the USDT leaves Ali's USDT jar. What arrives in euros is split two ways: 8,820.00 into the client's EUR jar at Ali, and 90.00 EUR into NPL's own jar at Ali (its pool). A new promise is made in euros, 8,820.00, which is what the client was quoted. The 90.00 is NPL's fee: this is the one moment client money becomes NPL's. Ali's own cost is hidden inside its rate, so it never appears as a line.
The arithmetic
  • 10,000.00 USDT × market 0.9000 = 9,000.00 EUR at market
  • fee 2.00 % of that = 180.00 (sender pays 1.40 % = 126.00, receiver pays 0.60 % = 54.00)
  • client net = 9,000.00 − 180.00 = 8,820.00 EUR → the client's jar and promise
  • Ali converts at its own rate 0.891: 10,000.00 × 0.891 = 8,910.00 EUR delivered (its 1.00 % cost is inside the rate, never a line)
  • NPL's margin = delivered 8,910.00 − client net 8,820.00 = 90.00 → NPL's pool at Ali

Pattern C — Settlement, confirmation, bank fees, shortfalls, rounding

Paying out: the partner sends the money, and from that moment it is out of NPL's hands, so the promise is treated as kept. The receiver's confirmation changes nothing unless it is short; a short delivery or a return brings the promise back for the missing part. Bank fees and rounding differences are settled according to the rules of the project. Bank transfers go out exact, cents included; rounding to a unit applies to cash hand-overs.
T7 · Settlement from Ali: 18,090.00 EUR released in two lines (Entity X1 8,000.00, Entity X2 10,090.00); the promise is discharged on release source: Settlement
AccountDebitCreditIn / OutCcyOwnerComponentWhy
CL.PROJECT.EUR.PAYABLE18,090.00IN · promiseEURclientprincipalonce the money has left Ali it is no longer in NPL's hands, so NPL no longer owes it; a return or a short delivery re-raises the promise
CL.ALI.EUR.DUE18,090.00OUT · moneyEURclientprincipalboth lines leave the client's balance at Ali; EUR bank transfers carry the cents (amount_rounding exact)
What is happening
Ali now pays out. The client's euro jar at Ali holds several converted deals, 18,090.00 in all; NPL sends it in two bank transfers, 8,000.00 to one of the receiver's accounts and 10,090.00 to another, cents included. The moment the money leaves Ali it is out of NPL's hands, so the promise is discharged right away: money OUT of the Ali jar, promise IN (fulfilled). The two transfers are now "sent"; the system remembers that on the payout lines, not in a jar. If one comes back, the promise comes back with it (T24).
The arithmetic
  • client euros at Ali: deal 1 8,820.00 + deal 2 4,410.00 + pass-through 4,860.00 = 18,090.00
  • paid out in full, cents included: 8,000.00 + 10,090.00 = 18,090.00; promise discharged by the same 18,090.00
T8 · Confirmation, line 1 full: Entity X1 received 8,000.00 EUR source: Confirmation
What is happening
The receiver confirms the first transfer arrived in full. Nothing posts: the promise was already discharged when the money was sent. The payout line just changes from "sent" to "confirmed", and a control checks that every sent line ends up confirmed, short or returned.
T9 · Confirmation, line 2 short: Entity X2 received 10,040.00 EUR, the bank took 50.00 (treatment carry_forward) source: Confirmation
AccountDebitCreditIn / OutCcyOwnerComponentWhy
CL.RECEIVER_X.EUR.SHORTFALL50.00IN · moneyEURclientbank_feestill owed to the receiver, topped up at the next settlement

The second transfer arrives 50.00 short because a bank took a fee on the way. The 10,040.00 that arrived needs no posting. For the missing 50.00 the promise is re-raised (NPL owes it again) and the amount is parked in a "shortfall" jar for that receiver, to be topped up next time. If the client had agreed to bear bank fees, nothing would post at all.
The arithmetic
  • line 2 was 10,090.00; received 10,040.00; bank fee 50.00 → shortfall jar, promise re-raised by 50.00

Sometimes money has to pass through one of NPL's own wallets: when the usual partner is unavailable, or when the sender can only pay on a network the partner does not accept. The ledger tracks it there until it leaves.

Ali is unavailable, so with management's approval the sender pays 10,000 USDT into NPL's own contingency wallet instead. The money goes IN to a "held in NPL's wallet" jar, and the usual promise goes OUT. Pattern E — Cancellations, reversals and voids

Two different things happen here. A mistake in the books (T18, T19): a posting was wrong, say a collection booked against the wrong deal. The ledger never lets anyone edit or delete it, because then yesterday's reports would no longer add up. Instead a second posting is made that is the exact mirror of the first and points at it, so the two cancel, and then the correct posting is made. Three entries stay on record: the wrong one, its cancellation, the right one. A cancelled deal (T20, T21): the money really did arrive, but the deal is called off, so the money has to go back to the sender. The promise to deliver is torn up and the money leaves the partner. Sending crypto costs a network fee; the project's rules say whether the sender bears it (the refund is a little smaller) or NPL does (the refund is whole and NPL books the fee as its cost). Because money has already moved, Management must approve how a cancelled deal is unwound.

Pattern F — Rejected payout and return leg

Pattern P — Interruptions between instruction and recording

What happens when something breaks in the middle: the partner has done its part but NPLify did not record it, a receiver never says the money arrived, or the blockchain explorer is down. The rule that keeps the books safe is simple: no automatic process ever moves value between jars. A person records what happened, once, with the evidence, and the system refuses to record the same event twice. So a breakdown can make a posting late; it can never make it double. The three cases and how each is resolved are set out in Pattern P of the Ledger Posting Design; the worked postings are added once the real behaviour has been exercised on staging.

Pattern Q — Month-end reconciliation with a partner statement

Once a month, and every evening during the parallel run, Finance puts the partner's statement next to the ledger. For each partner and currency the partner holds one balance; NPL's books say the same money is partly the client's jar and partly NPL's pool jar, and the two jars must add up to the partner's figure. When they do not, Finance finds out why before anything is posted: a payout the partner has already sent but the receiver has not yet confirmed (a timing difference, nothing to post), a charge the partner took and did not advise (a true-up, below), or a mistake on the partner's side (a query to the partner, nothing to post). Each reconciliation is kept as a record with the figures, the cause and who resolved it.

Pattern R — Opening entries on the opening date

On the day NPLify takes over, the money is already where it is: the client's euros are waiting at Ali, NPL's fees are resting there too, a rebate is owed by Jeton, a sender has a credit. Rather than re-typing months of old deals to arrive at those figures, Finance lists every such balance from the sheets, checks each one against the partner's statement or the wallet, Management approves the list, and each line is posted once as an opening entry with the statement attached. The jars start full, and everything that happens afterwards is an ordinary posting on top. The list is never a lump sum per partner: one line per receiver group and per sender, so the entitlement of each group and the balance of each sender start right; Finance adds the lines up three ways — per partner against the partner's statement, per group against the sheet, per sender against the sheet — before Management approves, and a line put under the wrong group shows up in the second and third sums even though the partner total still agrees.

Pattern G — inter-group offsets, and what is not here

One pattern of the main document has no table because it has no special lines: when a receiver of one group is paid out of money collected for another group, the payout posts exactly like Pattern C, and a report (not an account) shows one group owed and the other holding its flow. The main document also carries the full chart of accounts, the identities that must hold after every posting, the reports built from the ledger, and who may post what.
