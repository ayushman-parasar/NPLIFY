What changed since v1.0 (ERD v4.0). A client above the project (D38) and a system-settings area with the reporting currency and platform pairs (D43, D44). Names follow the FRS and NPL's words: PARTNER_ENTITY (was FACILITATING_ENTITY) owns COLLECTION_RECEIVING_ENDPOINT (replaces WALLET and COLLECTION_RECEIVING_ENTITY — D15, D18); DESTINATION_APPROVAL (was SETTLEMENT_REGISTRATION); MARKUP_SHARE_PARTY and MARKUP_SHARE_RULE (were INTRODUCER and REFERRAL_RULE — D37); disclosure and pricing are fee_in_rate / fee_on_market, the rate basis market / agent_rate. Fee structures gained the customer-facing figures (D22) and tiers (D21); overrides carry their own split (D20). The partner's clock — cutoff, time zone, holidays, overrides — and needs_bank_lock on the pair (D29, D34). Rounding became a per-pair menu on both sides with exact as the bank default (D25, D46). Own wallets have a role (D23, D40); endpoints and accounts have a risk band and a single-use form (D31, D32); wallet screenings are recorded (D31); templates are configuration (D36). Rebates are a percentage of the amount with a payout currency (D27, D46). The return group is gone: refunds use group-less sender_return receivers (D13).

1 · How configuration is organised

Four ideas carry the whole model. A client has projects. The client is the commercial relationship; each project is one arrangement with it, with its own settings, currency pairs, senders, receiver groups and partner configurations. Parties are partner-independent. Senders, receiver groups and receiving entities belong to the project, so the same Sender A → Receiver Y flow can run through Ali today and Jeton tomorrow. How a partner is used is a block. Fees and tiers, vehicles and their collection endpoints, the narrative and the rail that pay out, the destination approvals and the rebate rule all hang off one PARTNER_CONFIG, and a deal leg stamps the block's rows it was priced and routed on. The partner's clock is the partner's. Cutoff, time zone, holidays and overrides belong to the global PARTNER and apply wherever a pair says it relies on the banking channel.

Change control. Configuration is versioned: thresholds, fee structures, tiers, templates, system settings and policies are new rows with an effective date, never edits. Finance proposes, Management approves (approver ≠ proposer, from the ROLE_RIGHT rows — D44), and every change is audited. Deals keep the versions they were priced on. Three rows are not approvals but audited records: a partner cutoff override (Finance, with the partner's agreement), a wallet screening (Operations) and a destination approval's status as reported by the partner.

2 · Global master data and system settings

CLIENT · Global

The commercial relationship: the counterparty NPL contracts with and reports on (Betfair, Evo, 66 Group, GDC …). A client has one or more PROJECTs — BF, BF Sub and BF Withdrawal are three arrangements with one client — each with its own fee structures, partners and policies. Client-level figures (balances at partners, entitlement, custody, earnings, rebates) are roll-ups over the client’s projects (CLIENT_POSITION), never stored; an optional exposure_ceiling caps the sum of what rests at partners across the client’s projects. v5.4 (D38).

FEE_STRUCTURE · Per partner pair, versioned

The versioned NPL client-side fee for a partner × pair. Stamped on the deal group at pricing. v5.7 (D46): the sender and receiver parts are each a percentage of the principal at the basis rate, never compounded — fee_sender_part = amount_in × rate × sender_pays_pct, fee_receiver_part = amount_in × rate × receiver_pays_pct, client_net = amount_in × rate × (1 − sender_pays_pct − receiver_pays_pct); the partner’s cost then comes out of NPL’s parts (fee_in_rate) or is booked separately (fee_on_market). Evo USDT → EUR: sender pays 1 % or 1.4 % by sender type, receiver always 0.6 %; EUR → EUR 2.8 % split 1.4 / 1.4. Target-out quotes apply the fee as a markup on the target: amount_in = target × (1 + sender_pays_pct) ÷ rate (v5.8, D46 amendment).

6 · Cross-entity rules the system enforces on save

  1. Every project belongs to one client; a project's exposure_ceiling never exceeds its client's; both are compared through REPORTING_VALUE, so a platform pair must exist for every currency that rests at a partner (D43).
  2. Every project has exactly one default receiver group, created by the system. A counterparty receiver is in exactly one group; sender_return and partner_transit receivers are group-less and never on a forward deal or an allow-list (D13).
  3. Every sender has an allow-list naming groups of the same project and a sender_return receiver for refunds; a sender with no allow-list cannot be chosen on a deal.
  4. Every receiving entity has at least one account; accounts are unique per entity, currency and rail; a bank account carries a bank-details reference, a wallet account a network and a checksum-valid address with rail = network (D14). A single-use account or endpoint names the one deal it serves (D32).
  5. A partner pair's currency pair is a project pair; partner_pricing agrees with the partner's disclosure_style; partner_fee_pct is present exactly for fee_on_market; needs_bank_lock requires the partner's bank_cutoff_time and cutoff_timezone (D29).
  6. A fee structure's sender and receiver parts add up to its pct; min_markup ≤ max_markup; a fixed customer-facing fee names its pct (D22); tiers have exactly one row at volume 0 (D21); a new version is a new row with a later effective_from.
  7. A partner configuration that will pay out needs at least one partner entity, one settlement sending entity (narrative) and one settlement rail. A bank endpoint cannot go active without a rail (D18); a wallet endpoint's network is one the partner offers (D39); an own-desk configuration needs an own wallet of role own_desk (D23).
  8. A currency pair needs at least one rate source with a unique priority before it is usable; nearest_n and round_up_n need a rounding unit; cash rounding needs its unit (D25). A platform pair has no project and ends in the reporting currency (D44).
  9. A destination approval points at an account of a receiving entity in the same project; a quote-first deal for a group needs at least one approved destination with the chosen partner (invariant 8). A wallet account's approval carries its screening (D31).
  10. A quote template prints sender-facing fields only (D36); a screening decision of accept_alternate lands only on an elevated-band endpoint (D31); a cutoff override is a one-day or a weekday rule, never both; an extension names the partner's agreement.
  11. quote_freshness_min is shorter than quote_validity_min; all percentages are stored as decimals; Σ markup shares of one base ≤ 100 % (D26); a rebate rule is pct_of_amount with a payout currency (D46).
  12. applicable_for_reroute, is_own_desk, exposure ceilings, thresholds, fee structures and tiers, templates, system settings and platform pairs are Management-approved changes; users and roles need a second Management user (D44).

8 · Import and export format

A project's configuration can be exchanged as the YAML or JSON document shown above: one file per project plus one for global master data and system settings. Codes are the keys; the system resolves them to ids on import and rejects the file with the list of broken rules from Section 6 rather than importing part of it. Exports carry the version and effective dates of every fee structure, tier, template and threshold so a file is a faithful snapshot of what deals were priced on. The same format is the one the cutover loads into the staging instance (Cutover & Opening Entries v5.9).
